# Lieutenant Fizz — Episode 1: The Cocoa Caper
The first episode of *Lieutenant Fizz* (arc one, working title *Invasion of the Zargs*). A Keen-style platformer and the first full test title for the engine (`ENGINE_SPEC.md`).

## Pitch
Billy, 14, has vanished. His cousin Ben, 10, finds a secret lab under their treehouse, a prototype spaghetti with meatballs flying saucer, and Billy's map with an X marked *Planet Zargoth*. He straps on his bicycle helmet and follows him.

## Story
- **Hero:** Ben "Lieutenant Fizz" Blaze, 10, neighbourhood tinkerer. Bicycle helmet, modified pogo stick, Fizz Blaster.
- **Ride:** the spaghetti with meatballs flying saucer: noodle-upholstered seat, breadstick yoke, meatball thrusters, marinara exhaust.
- **Mission:** the Zargs are stealing Earth's cocoa beans to refill Zargoth's drying chocolate rivers. Billy went after them and was captured.
- **Planet Zargoth:** shining, twinkling crystals, chocolate rivers, mechanical hover platforms, frozen chocolate spikes, cake buildings with cookie doors.
- **Nemesis:** Mildred McMire, 10, Mortimer's cousin and best friend, building the galaxy's largest chocolate castle to live in.
- **Ending:** beat Mildred's Cocoa Colossus, take the gold USB drive, hack the citadel security terminal, free Billy.

See `STORY.md` for the full opening cinematic and ending text.

## Structure
1. Title screen (with a looping attract backdrop over Crater Fields, Crystal Caves and Mildred's Citadel, and Ben on the wordmark), then the opening cinematic (8 scenes, skippable).
2. **Overworld:** a top-down map (north is up; trees, rocks and buildings stand up on their tiles with a shadow, a slight tilt, and Ben walks behind them; every level has its own landmark on its entrance, and each has a pole that is bare until the level is cleared, when Ben throws a flag onto it) cut into four regions by chocolate rivers, each with its own ground, trees and levels. In Crater Fields the three levels open in order, tutorial first, and a small town, Crater Corners (houses, a shop, a fountain and three signposts that say a line when Ben walks up to them), stands around the watchtower, Zarg Lookout, the third level. Teleporter pairs link the regions and power up once the levels in front of them are cleared; the Citadel stays locked until the two Frosting Frontier levels before it are done. Autosaves on every visit. Back from a level, Ben stands at that level's doorstep with its prompt up, so one press tries it again; a press in the first half second is ignored, so the one that closed the card cannot open the level again by itself. A lake at the centre holds an island that cannot be walked to (see **The secret**).
3. **Areas and levels** (15 levels; the `Type` says how a level plays, not where it is):

3c. **The Saucer** (Crater Fields, walk-through): Ben's own flying saucer, parked at the map's starting point. Press Confirm at it to step inside: a short walk to the hatch, then a galley, bunks, a star chart, a souvenir shelf and a bridge up a ladder, with a painting at each that says something about the crew. No enemies, no snacks and no cleared bit; the exit at the far left goes straight back to the map. It has the id after the last cleared-level id, so it is in the level table but never in the progress mask.
4. **The secret.** The island in the lake, its teleporter pad and its level are visible from the start but cannot be walked to. One hidden room in the Sugar Glass Gallery holds a mural of the crystal forest with a ring of trees glowing in the far north-east. Stepping into it sets the secret-found flag, which makes a hidden teleporter pad appear inside a ring of trees in that corner of the map (the ring has one gap). It pairs with the island pad.
5. Ending (4 scenes), then the credits (skippable), then a score card. Episode 1 has no post-credits stinger; it belongs to Episode 3.

## Mechanics
- **Run & jump:** variable-height jump (release early to cut it short), 7 tiles/s top speed, momentum on the ground and in the air.
- **Pogo:** toggled on and off. Bounces automatically; hold jump for a high bounce (~6.6 tiles, adjustable via the pogoHeight setting). Stomping enemies while pogoing bounces higher.
- **Fizz Blaster:** cream soda bubbles. Stuns enemies for 6 s, never kills. Shots pass through enemies that are already stunned, so they reach the next one (a pass does not refresh the stun, shows a tiny cyan spark where the shot enters, and a shot that starts inside a stunned enemy just passes); bosses and invulnerable enemies still stop a shot. Aim up with ↑, or down with ↓ while in the air. Uses ammo; cream soda cans give +5.
- **Stomp:** landing on most enemies stuns them for 3 s.
- **Lives:** start with 3, extra life every 100 snack points. Death returns Ben to the map; game over offers your last save.
- **Hidden rooms and shortcuts:** three levels hide something for climbers, each hinted at by a wall painting near its foot. Crystal Caves has a ceiling room (vine up to a shelf, shoot the cracked wall) holding cookies, fudge and soda. Fudge Bog has the same inside a rock outcrop over its first dry land. Mirror Shafts has a deliberate shortcut: from the ledge under gate two, a cracked wall in the right-hand rock opens onto a vine that climbs inside the wall and comes out on top of the gate, skipping puzzle two and paying two cookies. A shortcut always costs a discovery and pays a reward; none skips a level's exit.
- **Pogo caches:** a small ledge about seven tiles above the ground holds a cookie and two cheezies in Crater Fields, Fudge Bog and Crystal Caves. A pogo bounce with Jump held reaches it; an ordinary jump (about three and a half tiles) cannot.
- **Jokes:** a few rare actions earn a one-off toast per level visit: thirty pogo bounces in a row, eight stomps on marshmallows, twelve wall-kicks, and twenty seconds of standing still. None changes the game.
- **Cameos:** a chalk drawing of Billy hides in the Crystal Caves room and one of Mortimer McMire (with a moustache added) in the Fudge Bog room. Touching a drawing shows its line; they are drawings, so they do not contradict Billy's capture.
- **Cracked walls:** a cracked rock tile is solid until a fizz shot from Ben hits it; it and every cracked tile touching it then crumble away. Enemy bubbles do nothing to it. Cracks hide short ways through to secret rooms, so a cracked wall is always a promise, never a trap.
- **Lanterns:** a hanging candy lantern is shootable scenery. A fizz bubble pops it into a few cheezies (the spawn's `dir` is the count, three by default).
- **Wall paintings:** a painted panel shows one line of lore the first time Ben touches it. The paintings hint at the world's history: the crystal growers, the round ship, fizz on a dry crack, and the long stair down.
- **Vines:** vine tiles are not solid. Up grabs one anywhere along its length, Up and Down move at 3.5 tiles/s, and Left and Right shuffle sideways at 3 tiles/s from vine to vine. Jump lets go with a hop, exactly as on a ladder. Vines suit cave walls, where a ladder would look out of place.
- **Mantle:** when Ben jumps and just misses a ledge (the top edge is within about one tile of his feet, there is room to stand on it and the way up the wall and over the lip is clear), he hauls himself up onto it automatically ("heave"). Level design can therefore count on a one-tile overshoot. The pull-up is not instant: it takes `MANTLE_TIME`, 0.30 s, which the sim spends hanging at the grab, rising straight up the wall for the first 60% (`MANTLE_RISE_SHARE`) and then stepping over the lip, with three Ben frames (grip, haul, push) following the progress; the heave sound plays at the grab. While it runs Ben cannot move, jump, pogo or fire, and gravity and collision do not act on him; he ends standing on the ledge at rest, exactly where the old instant pull-up put him. Anything that hurts him still does, and any hit is fatal: the mantle cancels and he tumbles from where he was hanging. A bounce off a marshmallow, touching the exit, or a switched tile closing on the path also ends it, and loading a level or the map starts without one. Pausing simply freezes it.
- **Wall-kick:** pressing Jump while touching a solid wall in mid-air kicks off it (up 19 tiles/s, away 6 tiles/s). Each side allows one kick until Ben lands, climbs, or kicks off the opposite wall, so a single wall cannot be scaled but two facing walls (a shaft at least three tiles wide) can. A kick is always full height.
- **Head bump:** when Ben is rising (faster than `HEAD_BUMP_MIN_SPEED`, 1 tile/s) and his head meets a solid tile, his upward speed stops as it always has and the sim raises the "clunk" caption once, at the top of his head. It is the helmet knocking on stone, felt as the phone's or pad's `clunk` tap and heard as the `clunk` sound (the same caption as a cookie door opening). A held Jump or Up against the ceiling, a fall from under it, walking under a low ceiling, climbing a ladder or vine into one, and a bounce with no room to rise are all silent, so each bump sounds once. On the pogo stick Ben is drawn 2 tiles tall against a 1.4-tile body, so while the stick is rising its ceiling check uses the drawn height (`POGO_DRAWN_HEIGHT`, behind the `POGO_HEAD_BOX` switch): a bounce stops with the drawn head under the slab, not inside it, and a full bounce under a ceiling gives up at most 0.6 tile of height. Walking, falling, standing, floors, walls and enemy hits use the 1.4-tile body as before, and a pogo can still start a bounce under a ceiling just 2 tiles above the floor. It is raised in a level only, never on the map.
- **Looking and aiming:** holding Up or Down while standing still turns Ben's eyes that way (and nudges the camera), and a shot fired up or down raises or lowers his blaster arm for a moment, in the air and on a ladder too.
- **Enemies and lifts:** enemies pass through lift trays as if they were not there, so a lift never carries one off. A few chosen enemies are riders instead and travel on a platform (a beetle rides the first hover platform in Crystal Caves); riders are exempt from the stray rule. A ground enemy that ends up more than four tiles from its home floor for three seconds fades back to where it started.
- **Ladders:** Up grabs a ladder, Up and Down move along it at 4.5 tiles/s, Jump lets go with a hop (Ben cannot grab the same ladder again until Up and Down are released or he leaves it), and Ben can fire while climbing: Fire alone shoots sideways, Up with Fire shoots up and Down with Fire shoots down. The top rung is a ledge: Ben stands on it and presses Down to climb back onto the ladder. Pogo is off while climbing.
- **Hidden rooms:** in theatre levels, painted flats cover some rooms completely. They fade to about a fifth opaque while Ben is inside and fade back when he leaves; everything inside, from snacks to ambushers, is hidden until then.
- **Mirrors and crystal switches:** a fizz bubble bounces off a 45-degree mirror (`/` sends an upward bubble right, `\` sends it left). A swivel mirror swings to the other angle after every bounce. A crystal switch opens its own gate when a bubble hits it; each gate has its own switch.
- **The secret:** one hidden room in the Sugar Glass Gallery counts as the secret. The first time Ben steps inside, the game records it (bit 15 of the saved progress) and toasts a clue pointing at the far north-east of the map.
- **Conveyor belts** carry Ben (and enemies) at 3 tiles/s in the direction of their chevrons, on top of his own running. **Presses** rise and fall over a floor on a fixed beat; one that is raised is harmless, one that is down crushes. **Molten metal** is lethal like fudge.
- **Keys:** red, blue and green gumdrops open matching cookie doors.

## Controls
| Action | Keen-style | Modern | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move | ← → | ← → / A D | D-pad / stick |
| Jump | Ctrl | Z | A (remappable) |
| Pogo | Alt | X | B / Y |
| Fizz | Space | C | X / RT |
| Menu | Esc / Pause | Esc / P / Pause | Start |
| Save / Load | F5 / F9 | F5 / F9 | Pause menu |
| Fullscreen | F | F | — |

- **Fullscreen:** F (or the round button at the top right) enters and leaves fullscreen where the browser allows it; it is not shown in the app or on an iPhone. It works whatever Options > Display > Fullscreen says, which only decides whether starting or resuming a run goes fullscreen by itself (on for touch devices, never on a desktop). On a phone the button shows on the title and the pause menu, beside Pause, and not in play. See [MOBILE.md](MOBILE.md#fullscreen-button-built-in-slice-76).
- **Esc in fullscreen:** where the browser offers the Keyboard Lock (Chromium), Esc still opens the pause menu from fullscreen, and on the top level of the pause menu or the title it leaves fullscreen (resume with the Resume row, P or Pause). Elsewhere the browser takes Esc to leave fullscreen and the level pauses. See [MOBILE.md](MOBILE.md#esc-and-the-pause-key-in-fullscreen).
- **Menus:** the title menu is New Game, Continue (it names the newest save), Load game, Options and Controls. The pause menu is Resume, Save game, Load game, Options, Leave level (in a level) and Quit to title.
- **Options:** Sound opens its own screen (see below); Haptics opens its own screen (see below) and appears only where the phone can vibrate or a pad that can rumble has been seen, or when `?haptics` is in the address; Controller opens its own screen (see below) and appears once a gamepad has been seen this session; Captions is On or Off; Controls picks which keyboard column the hints and the Controls table show (Keen-style or Modern; both layouts always work); Text size is Normal or Large (one scale step up); Motion is System, Reduced or Full. Left and right change a value, and Enter steps it. They are saved on this device.
- **Options > Sound:** a screen over Options with Style (Classic or Enhanced; it starts on Auto, which shows the game's default, and stepping saves a choice), Music and Effects (8-block meters, the old Options Sound row renamed), Music version (Classic or Enhanced; it starts on Auto, which shows Enhanced, and stepping saves a choice: Classic is the game's own loops and Enhanced plays each level, overworld and boss cue as a song of about a minute, with depth, and the other cues as they are), Effects version (Classic or Enhanced; it starts on Auto, which shows Enhanced, and stepping saves a choice: Classic is the game's own effects and Enhanced adds pitch curves, bell glints and a filter that opens at each attack, each effect at about the same length and level), Sound lab (Off or On, off by default; On adds the sound-lab button for tuning by ear, which `?debug` also shows), Reset (a second press within three seconds puts Style, Music, Music version, Effects, Effects version and the Sound lab back and changes nothing else) and Back. A step on Style, Effects or Effects version plays a short sample so the change can be heard (a boing then a key for Effects version), and the music is never muffled on this screen, even when it is opened from the pause menu. `?audio=classic` or `?audio=enhanced` overrides the saved style for the session without saving it, and the Style row then shows `Classic (link)` or `Enhanced (link)`, dimmed and unchangeable. `?debug` puts the sound lab on, so the Sound lab row then shows `On (link)`, fixed.
- **Options > Haptics:** a screen over Options with Strength (Off, Light, Medium or Strong; Strong at first; stepping buzzes the phone with a jump at the new strength), Rumble (the same four levels for a controller; shown once a pad that can rumble has been seen; stepping rumbles the pad), Haptics lab (Off or On, off by default; `?debug` shows the lab too), Reset (a second press within three seconds puts Strength and Rumble on Strong and the lab Off, and changes nothing else) and Back. Haptics are on by default where a device can use them. `?haptics` forces them on and `?haptics=off` forces them off for the visit without saving; Strength and Rumble then show `On (link)` or `Off (link)` and cannot be changed, and `?debug` fixes the Haptics lab row the same way. See [MOBILE.md](MOBILE.md#haptics).
- **Options > Play sample (planned):** a screen over Options for recording a play session to help playtesting. It is off by default. The first screen lists in plain words everything that is recorded (every key, button and touch with where and when, including touches that miss and touches in the menus; where Ben is; the screens visited; the game's events; the screen size, browser and operating system; where the touch controls sit; and the date, time and time zone the session started) and everything that is not (name, account, device ID, location, anything outside the game), says it is saved on the device as you play, and says nothing is sent anywhere. Start sample begins at the next screen change and a small recording marker shows with Stop sample. When a sample is stopped, or an earlier one is found at launch, a review screen shows what is in the file, its size and its specs, with Watch (when the sample has a level in it), Save zip, Share (where the system has a share menu) and Delete. Saved samples are only deleted by the player. **Open a zip**, at the foot of Saved samples, lets the player pick a sample zip someone sent them (or one they saved earlier) and watch it: it is read on the device from the one file picked, shown, and not kept. **Watch** plays the sample back: the game re-runs the level with the player's own presses, on a copy of the game that touches nothing of the real one (no saves, no new sample, no change to the map or the level in progress), so the player sees what they did. A bar has Play and Pause, Last try and Next try, Last death and Next death (each a moment and a half before the loss), a speed button (0.5x, 1x, 2x, 4x), a scrub bar, a Layers sheet and Back. The layers are Touches (where the controls were and where each finger landed: a touch on a control is a filled blue disc, a miss a pink ring with a cross), Path, Stays (where Ben stayed 1.5 seconds or more), Deaths (a cross with the cause), Other tries at the same level, Buttons (the buttons held) and Sound (the game's sound effects as they happened; music stays as it was). On a keyboard or pad, Jump or Enter plays and pauses, Left and Right skip five seconds, Up and Down go by deaths, Fizz changes the speed, and Esc, Pause or the pogo button goes back. If the replay stops matching what was recorded (the game has changed since, for example) a note says where, and what follows is the game's own run.
- **Options > Controller:** a screen over Options (from the title or the pause menu) for the gamepad's buttons, with Jump, Pogo, Fizz and Pause (each shows its buttons by their name on the controller in use, such as `B, Y` or `Circle, Triangle`), Reset to defaults (two presses within three seconds put every action back on A, B and Y, X and RT, and Start) and Back. The row on Options reads Default or Custom and exists only once a gamepad has been seen this session (it stays once shown); it is not shown for `?debug` alone. An action can have several buttons (Pogo is B and Y, Fizz is X and RT, by default). Choosing an action says "Press buttons for Pogo, wait when done" and collects presses: buttons held when it began are ignored until released, and the D-pad and sticks never count. The first press replaces the action's buttons with that one, each further press adds a button (or takes it out again if it is already collected, though the last one cannot be taken out), and the row shows what has been collected so far ("B, Y …"). The listen finishes by itself 1.5 seconds after the last press, or at once with Enter or a tap on the row, and only then is anything saved. If another action had one of the buttons, it loses it and keeps its others; if that would leave it with nothing it keeps the button too, so the button is shared, and the message says so. Esc, Back, holding Start for a second (a shorter Start press counts as a press of Start), or eight seconds without a first press cancels the whole listen, leaving the old buttons, and the screen stays; so does a tap before any button has been pressed. While listening, the pad does nothing else. Bindings are saved on this device under `lf-pad-bindings-v1` and apply at once. Menu Select and Back are the Jump (or Fizz) and Pogo buttons, so they follow the change; the D-pad and stick are fixed, and so are Esc and the keyboard (keyboard remapping is not offered). The hints, the Controls screen and the credits and stinger hints show the new buttons. Rows are the same height as every other menu, 40 dp on a touch screen with room. See [ENGINE_SPEC.md](ENGINE_SPEC.md#7-input).
- **Save slots:** the autosave (read-only), four slots and a quick-save (F5 writes it, F9 loads it, and the Load game screen lists it; the Save game screen does not). Each shows a mini overworld with Ben's position, the area, a pip per level cleared, lives, score, time played and the date. Esc or B goes back from any screen opened over a menu.
- **Hints:** keycap and button hints along the bottom of each screen follow the last input used: pressing a key shows keyboard hints, touching the gamepad (or connecting one) shows button hints (named for the controller, A/B/X/Y on an Xbox or unknown pad, Cross/Circle/Square/Triangle on a PlayStation one, and the buttons the player has chosen), and unplugging the last pad goes back to the keyboard.
- **Mouse cursor:** in play (a level or the map) the cursor hides after the mouse rests for 1.5 seconds and comes back as soon as it moves, so a keyboard or gamepad player does not see it. It stays on every screen the mouse can click (title, pause, cards, dialogue, cinematics and the rest) and over the debug panel and the labs. Touch never shows one.

## Art direction
- 16-colour EGA palette; bright and fun, in the style of *Goodbye, Galaxy!* and *Aliens Ate My Babysitter*.
- Daylight by default, with Last Light's lighting effects layered on top. A night variant is supported (§5.1 of the engine spec).
- UI uses the Liminal HQ design system's colours, scrims and panels in the DOM overlay, set in the Fizz pixel font at whole-pixel sizes (`docs/FONT.md`); the world stays strictly pixel art. The title and pause menus are left-aligned lists over a scrim, with a soda-can bullet and an orange stepped plate on the selected row, and the pause and card screens hide the HUD.

## Audio
- **Music:** 16 looping tracks: title, map, boss, ending, four for the opening cinematic (`yard`, `lab`, `launch`, `cine`), and one per level type: `crater`, `caves`, `citadel`, `sky` (open sky), `tower` (buildings), `theatre` (a waltz), `foundry` and `secret` (Gumdrop Isle). Mirror Shafts reuses `caves` and Fudge Bog reuses `crater`.
- **Sound effects:** about 30, tied to the in-world sound captions.
- **Implementation:** written for Undertone; see `ENGINE_SPEC.md` §6.
