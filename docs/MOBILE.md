# Mobile: touch controls, the phone layout and haptics

**Status: partly built.** The in-level touch controls, the glass HUD, the phone viewport and menus on the controls exist (slices 0 to 4 and the title, the Controls touch column, the Back button and the browser Back guard of slice 5 in [MOBILE_PLAN.md](MOBILE_PLAN.md)), and so do the stored touch settings and the Touch controls screen for size, opacity and hand (slices 6.1 and 6.2), and so does the drag editor that moves the controls (6.3); the haptics core, controller rumble and the Options > Haptics screen are in and on by default where a vibrator or a pad exists (see "Web haptics" under Haptics), and the rest is planned (see "Mobile and touch controls" in `docs/STATUS.md`). This document follows `design/Mobile Design.dc.html` (turn 3) and plans the touch controls, a layout tailored to phones and haptics. It applies to the website on a phone browser as much as to the Tauri app in [APP.md](APP.md), because all of it is web UI in the shared engine and episode packages.

The build order and the code changes are in [MOBILE_PLAN.md](MOBILE_PLAN.md).

## How to read the artboards
The artboards are about the **UI**: layout, controls, spacing and type. They are not a spec for game content, and they estimate it (the cinematic panel count, the level and score values, the art behind a screen). Where an artboard and the game disagree on content, **the current implementation wins**. The 3a screens use the Fizz font faithfully, and Fizz is the type for every phone screen.

## Where the phone title and the launcher meet
- **On the web (a phone browser):** the episode shows the mobile title screen below, with its own New game, Continue, Load game and Controls.
- **In the Tauri app:** the launcher in APP.md replaces that title, and the episode starts embedded, straight into play. The mobile title is bypassed.
- Everything after the title (cinematic, map, level, pause, score card, game over) is the same in both.

## Principles
- **Phones are landscape.** The game is played with two thumbs on a landscape screen. Portrait shows a "rotate your phone" screen on the website and is locked out in the app.
- **All text is Fizz.** The Fizz type rule (font size is 11px × a whole number n) and the integer pixel scale of the game canvas both hold on phones. Where the device pixel ratio is not an integer, the scale is chosen so the pixels stay square, and the slack becomes a slightly taller view (more of the level), never bars or a stretch.
- **Nothing important sits under a thumb or a notch.** Controls and HUD respect the display cutout and the system gesture areas.
- **No new rules in the sim.** Touch is another input source and haptics are another output. Both are presentation, so the simulation stays deterministic.

## Display
- Landscape only, fullscreen in the app.
- The game renders full-bleed, under the display cutout and the system bars (the viewport uses `viewport-fit=cover`, and `ui.css` defines `--lf-safe-*` from the `env(safe-area-inset-*)` values). Controls, the HUD, menus and hints stay inside the safe area, using the CSS `env(safe-area-inset-*)` values, which work in browsers and in the Tauri webview.
- The play field is drawn at a whole pixel scale on touch (slice 4): the canvas is backed by device pixels and each sprite pixel is a whole number of them, with the leftover height showing more of the level. Controls are drawn over it, translucent, because a phone has no spare margin.
- **Pixels: Sharp or Soft** (an option, see APP.md) decides what happens to the slack: Sharp keeps whole pixels and shows a slightly taller view, Soft stretches to the edges. Touch defaults to Sharp and desktop to Soft; `?pixels=sharp|soft` overrides it until the option is built.
- **Fast (slice 6b.2, `?pixels=fast`)** is Sharp with one canvas pixel per sprite pixel that the browser scales up by the same whole factor, for slower GPUs. The view is the same as Sharp's (the same tiles and framing on the title, in a level, on the map and in a cinematic), with about 9 to 25 times fewer pixels shaded on a phone. The cost is that a few device pixels at the right or bottom can be cropped, scrolling and moving sprites step a whole sprite pixel at a time and lighting is per sprite pixel. There is no Options row for it yet.

## Touch controls (design 2a, chosen)
- **Move:** a round glass D-pad on the left thumb. Left and right move, and up and down aim the Fizz Blaster (as ↑ and ↓ do on the keyboard). It maps one to one to the gamepad, so help text works for both.
- **Actions** on the right thumb: **Jump** is the largest and sits where the thumb rests, **Pogo** is a small round button above it, and **Fizz** is a round button that shows the ammo count (the "5" on it).
- **HUD:** three glass pills at the top left, in the Fizz font: lives, snacks in yellow and fizz in cyan, matching the desktop scoreboard. **Pause** is always visible at the top right.
- **Controller connected:** the designs include a "Controller connected" state. Hints switch to pad labels when a gamepad is the last device used, as they do today; what the on-screen controls do in that state (hide or dim) is an open question below.
- **Sound screen** (reached from Options): Options opens it from its first row, so Options was eight rows on a touch device that can vibrate (Sound, Haptics, Captions, Controls, Text size, Motion, Touch controls, Back), and is nine with the Display row below. Nine rows at 48 dp do not fit a landscape phone, so the list scrolls (see "Long menus scroll" below). The Sound screen has six rows (Style, Music, Effects, Sound lab, Reset, Back; a row the address fixes shows "(link)", below); a tap on a row steps it, the ◄ ► steppers step down and up, Reset takes two taps within three seconds, and the Back button closes it onto the Sound row. It follows `?title=split` and Left-handed like the other menus.
- **Touch controls screen** (reached from Options): on a touch device Options has a **Touch controls** row before Back (a device is touch when `?touch` is set, the pointer is coarse at start, or it has been touched this session; the row never disappears again, and a desktop Options screen does not have it). It shows Size (Small, Medium or Large), Opacity (40, 60, 85 or 100 percent, 85 by default), Left-handed (the whole layout mirrors), Move controls (the drag editor, below) and Reset. Haptics used to be a row here; it has its own screen under Options now (below), measured at 48 dp, 47 and 41 in the split menus for these six rows. Size and Left-handed show at once on the controls behind the menu; Opacity applies in play and in the editor, and menus stay solid. Reset takes a second tap within three seconds (any move or a lapse cancels it) and puts size, opacity, hand and the moved controls back; the haptic strength has its own Reset and is left alone. Every change is saved at once in `lf-touch-v1`.
- **Haptics screen** (reached from Options): Options has a **Haptics** row under Sound, whose value is the phone's strength in effect. It appears only where there is something to feel: a touch device whose browser has `navigator.vibrate`, a pad with a vibration actuator seen this session (it never disappears again), or `?haptics` in the address. A desktop with a keyboard and no pad does not have it. The screen has Strength (Off, Light, Medium or Strong, which scale every phone cue by 0, 0.5, 0.75 and 1), Rumble (the same four levels for a controller, default Strong; shown only once a pad that can rumble has been seen), Haptics lab (Off or On, default Off; the lab itself is described under Haptics), Reset (a second tap within three seconds puts Strength and Rumble on Strong and the lab Off, and changes nothing else) and Back. Stepping Strength plays a jump at the new strength on the phone, and stepping Rumble plays a bonk on the pad, so the change is felt; Strength Off sends nothing to the phone, menus included. It has four rows without a pad (48 dp in the split menus at 390, 360 and 320 dp tall, 48, 48 and 44 in the one-column fallback) and five with one (48, 48 and 48 split; 48, 43 and 35 in the column). Strength is saved as `hapticStrength` in `lf-touch-v1` (still `v: 1`: a save from before it, with `haptics: false`, reads as Off, anything else as Strong, and a `hapticStrength` that is not 0 to 3 is Strong), because it is the phone's setting, like the rest of that key; Rumble and the lab switch are saved as `rumble` and `hapticsLab` in `lf-ep1-options-v1`.
- **Display screen** (reached from Options, built in slice 7.4): Options has a **Display** row under Sound and Haptics, whose value is the Fullscreen setting in effect. The screen has Fullscreen (Auto, On or Off; Auto is on for touch devices and off on desktop), Keep screen on (On or Off, On by default) and Back, at the chosen row height in the split menus. On a touch device it also has **Row spacing** (Compact 36 dp, Cozy 40 dp, Comfy 48 dp; Cozy by default; saved as `density`), which resizes the menu rows at once. A row appears only where the page can do it: Fullscreen needs element fullscreen in a browser page (not on an iPhone; in the Tauri desktop app it is the native window's fullscreen, and Android is always fullscreen natively) and Keep screen on needs a wake lock in a secure context (not over plain LAN http), and Options has no Display row when neither exists (a touch device always has Row spacing, so it always has the Display row). Fullscreen is read each time a run starts or resumes; turning it Off never leaves fullscreen. Both settings are saved in `lf-ep1-options-v1` as `fullscreen` (0 Auto, 1 On, 2 Off) and `awake`. With Display, Options is nine rows on a touch device that can vibrate, measured at 35 dp in the split menus at 390 dp tall, 31 at 360 and 27 at 320 (27, 24 and 22 in the one-column fallback).
- **Rows the address decides.** `?audio=classic` or `=enhanced` fixes Style, `?haptics` (or `=on`) and `?haptics=off` fix Strength and Rumble, `?fullscreen` (or `=on`) and `?fullscreen=off` fix Fullscreen, `?wake` (or `=on`) and `?wake=off` fix Keep screen on, and `?debug` puts the Sound lab and the Haptics lab on whatever their options say. A row the address fixes shows its value with "(link)" (`Classic (link)`, `On (link)`, `Off (link)`), is dimmed, has no steppers, is skipped when moving through the rows and does not change when tapped. The link is never saved: the player's own choice stays in storage for when the link is gone. A screen opens on its first row that can change.
  - **Moving the controls:** the Move controls row opens an editor over the dimmed game. The D-pad, Jump, Pogo and Fizz show a dashed outline and drag with a finger; the control stays under the thumb where it was grabbed. A control stays on its own side of the screen, below a 96 px band at the top (the HUD pills, the boss and toast lines and Pause live there), inside the safe area, and leaves menus a column of at least 300 px; it cannot be dropped on another control and is pushed off to an 8 px gap. Pause cannot be moved. A bar at the top has **Done** (where Back sits), the heading "Move controls" and **Reset**, with "Drag a control to move it" under it; Reset takes two taps and puts the controls back (the other settings stay). Every drop is saved at once, and Left-handed mirrors the moved layout. Positions are stored as the distance from the control's own corner of the screen, so they hold across rotation and notches, and the Size setting scales them. If the saved positions cannot be placed (they overlap, say) the whole layout uses the default places until they are changed. While a control is raised, the prompt in a level moves in from that side to stay clear of it.
  - **Height without the browser bar:** in fullscreen or an installed app the default places of the four controls are raised by about 52 dp (less on shorter windows), so they sit at the same height from the bottom of the phone as they did with the browser's bar showing. Controls the player has moved keep their saved place in the window; see `docs/MOBILE_PLAN.md`.
- **Input:** touch events feed the same per-tick edge detection as keys and pad buttons in the `InputManager`, so the sim sees one input stream. Multi-touch matters: moving and jumping happen together. Touch becomes a third device for the hint system, which already tracks keyboard and gamepad.

**Not in the first build** (designed, kept for later): 2b split arrows with a rising row of actions, 2c a pogo-switch pill with two large buttons (pogo is already a toggle, so it fits), and 1b the floating joystick with EGA plate buttons and a Keen-style scoreboard HUD, and 1c the thumb arc. The designs say the D-pad and the floating joystick will both end up as options in Settings.

## The phone layout
- **Title (web only):** "Ben Blaze in" over the Lieutenant Fizz wordmark and "Episode 1 · The Cocoa Caper" on the left, and the menu on the thumb side (right): New game, Continue (with the slot, such as "Slot 2"), Load game, Controls. Two layouts exist. The default is the desktop column with the D-pad gutter on the left (the game shows to the right of it): the wordmark is one scale smaller on a phone under 420 px tall, the rows take the column's width, and the text is outlined by one glyph pixel so it reads over the game. Its rows are 38 px at 844×390 and 35 px at 740×360, since 48 dp does not fit five rows under the wordmark. The opt-in split layout (`?title=split`, planned as a setting) puts the logo on the D-pad side above the D-pad and the menu on the thumb side with 48 px rows; when the logo cannot fit (Large text on a short phone) it falls back to the column. The Controls screen keeps the room between the controls in both, and in the split layout it sits on the thumb side.
- **Split menus (opt-in, `?title=split`, slice 6.4):** the other menus follow the split title. The heading sits on the D-pad side at the top (below the Back button when one shows) and the text, rows and hints on the thumb side, so more rows show at once than in the column; every row is 48 dp in both layouts and a list that does not fit scrolls; a card keeps one 48 px row beside its text. Left-handed mirrors it, with the heading below the Pause button on the pause menu. A screen whose heading cannot fit above the D-pad (Large text, a raised D-pad) uses the one column. The column stays the default; whether the split should be is an open question.
- **Menus** use the desktop scrim style: a list over a soft scrim, a stepped orange plate that slowly cycles colour, and a can of cream soda as the cursor. The **on-screen controls stay up and drive the menus like a gamepad** (the D-pad moves, Jump is relabelled Select, Pogo is Back), so the thumbs never leave them; menu content starts right of the D-pad on touch, and a held direction auto-repeats. On a menu Select and Back act when the finger lifts inside the button (so a press can be cancelled by sliding off), while the buttons in play act on the press. Taps work too as the backup: rows are **48 dp tall** where the screen has room (see Android guidelines; a landscape phone 360 to 390 dp tall fits most menus only with shorter rows, so the rows take the height that fits), Option rows have ◄ and ► steppers, and a tap moves the cursor to the row and chooses it, as a tap on any Android list does. The design's artboards show a select-then-choose tap; that is replaced here so the app behaves the way Android users expect. Destructive rows (Reset, Quit) still ask for a second tap.
- **Controls screen and Back (slice 5b):** on touch the Controls screen shows two columns, Action and Touch, naming the on-screen controls (the D-pad moves and aims, the Jump, Pogo and Fizz buttons, Pause, and the D-pad, Select and Back on menus, with Save / Load in the Pause menu), with no keyboard or gamepad keys. A **← Back** button at the top left (at least 48 dp, inside the safe area) closes Controls, Options, Sound, Touch controls and Saves one level, from the title or the pause menu, and also shows on the pause menu itself, where it resumes (it is hidden on the title's top level, in play and in the touch editor). The on-screen **Pause** button stays up on every one of those screens and leaves the menu entirely: over the pause menu it closes every screen opened over it (at any depth, such as Options › Sound) and resumes the game; over the title it closes them back to the title's top-level menu, with the row that opened the first one selected. On the pause menu's own top level it still resumes. Esc, P, the Pause key and a gamepad's Start keep going back one level from a screen opened over a menu. It never shows on a desktop. The browser's Back button is handled separately (slice 5c): in fullscreen or an installed app, or with `?back` for testing, Back opens the pause menu in a level, resumes from it, closes Options, Saves and Controls, skips a cinematic or the credits, and on the title with nothing open leaves the page. Fullscreen arrives with slice 7, so on a phone browser this stays off until then.
- **Cinematic and ending:** the opening cinematic and the ending are scenes whose text arrives a page at a time: the authored beats are packed at runtime into pages of up to three lines (two on a screen where three would take more than about 30 % of the height; 844×390 and 740×360 get three, 640×320 two; see "Story beats" in `MOBILE_PLAN.md`). A page advances on tap (on the text, on the arrow button, or anywhere off the buttons). A tap finishes the typed page, and another tap moves to the next page, then the next scene; a turn or a resize packs the pages again and keeps your place. The dots count scenes. On a phone the top bar floats over the art (with the place, the dots and Skip), and the bottom is a strip: the page's lines with a 48 px **↓** button beside them, at most about 30 % of the screen height with three lines and 25 % with two (27.2, 29.4 and 23.7 % at 844×390, 740×360 and 640×320). The button that leaves the intro keeps the word "Step out". A desktop window keeps the opaque bars, the word "Continue" and the dots in the foot. A Skip button is always there in the intro; the ending has none.
- **Overworld map:** the same D-pad as a level. Walking next to a level opens a card with its name, a line about it and an "Enter level" button.
- **The map's card docks away from Ben (phones only).** The card for a level, a sign or a teleporter sits against the edge of the screen opposite Ben, so he and the path beside him stay in view, and wraps to more lines in the narrower column (the text size is unchanged). It flips to the other edge when Ben is clearly past the middle of the screen, and stays put while he is near it. It keeps clear of the D-pad and the Jump, Fizz and Pogo buttons wherever they are (Left-handed, Large or moved), the HUD pills, Pause and the safe area, and takes no touches. On the shortest phones (640×320) the longest texts cannot fit between the controls; the card then steps onto the top of a control rather than onto Ben. The desktop panel is unchanged.
- **In level:** the HUD and controls above.
- **Pause menu:** the scrim menu over the frozen level: Resume, Save game (with the slot), Load game, Controls, Leave level, Quit to title. On touch, the Back button at the top left also resumes from it. The Back gesture opens it in a level (in the browser, the Back button does so in fullscreen or an installed app, or with `?back`).
- **Score card (pending):** "<Level> cleared" with totals that count up (Snacks, Fizz fired, Zargs stunned, Secrets, Time, Best time) and one clear next step, "Back to the map". **Pending:** none of Fizz fired, Zargs stunned, Secrets or Best time are tracked today, so the card first ships with the totals the game already has, and the rest follow once the shell or sim counts them.
- **Game over:** calm, with your last save offered first.
- **Dialogue and cards** advance on tap anywhere as well as with buttons.
- The e2e layout checks gain phone-sized landscape viewports with touch emulation (for example 844×390 and 740×360) and the same rules: nothing overflows, clips or sits under a control, and all pixel text is `11 × n` in Fizz.

## Android guidelines
The phone UI follows Android's guidelines for the best experience, which in a WebView (where one CSS pixel is one dp) means:
- **Touch targets are at least 48 × 48 dp, with at least 8 dp between targets.** That covers menu rows, every on-screen control (the glass buttons and the D-pad's arms), the pause button, the Skip button and the Touch controls panel chips. Visuals may be smaller than the hit area, but the hit area is never smaller. This replaces the artboards' 44 px rows. Menu rows are the exception: they are 40 dp by default (Row spacing offers 36 and 48), a deliberate trade of target height for more rows on a short phone, and a menu too tall for the screen scrolls rather than shrinking its rows.
- **Gesture navigation.** Android's back and home gestures start at the screen edges, so controls sit inset from the edges, and the app registers system gesture exclusion rects for the D-pad and the buttons so a thumb sliding near them does not trigger Back. The Back gesture itself goes through predictive back (the `predictive-back` plugin) and opens the pause menu in a level.
- **Edge to edge with cutouts.** Draw behind the system bars, and keep the HUD and controls inside the display cutout and gesture insets (`env(safe-area-inset-*)` on the web, the window insets in the app).
- **Accessibility.** Menus and cards are real DOM controls with names, roles and focus order, so TalkBack and Switch Access can drive them. The on-screen game controls have content descriptions. Text and controls meet contrast of 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text and graphics). Honour the system's "remove animations" setting through the Motion option, and the system font and display size through Text size (the minimum stays n = 2).
- **Haptics** for menus are game-lane patterns played at the Strength setting, not the system's feedback constants (see Haptics).
- **Large screens and foldables.** On Android 16 and later, apps that target API 36 or higher ignore a landscape-only lock on large screens (600 dp and wider), so the layout adapts to any window size instead of assuming a locked orientation, and works in split-screen and resizable windows.
- **Audio and lifecycle.** Request audio focus, pause when it is lost (a call) and when the app is backgrounded, and play on the media volume.

## Long menus scroll
Menu rows are `--lf-menu-row` tall on touch: 40 dp by default (Cozy), or 36 (Compact) or 48 (Comfy) from Options > Display > Row spacing; the three heights are one table in `menu-density.ts`. A menu that does not fit the height its screen leaves it scrolls inside its own area, always showing whole rows (its height is cut to a whole number of rows, never fewer than two, and it comes to rest on a row boundary after a drag, a fling or the wheel), while the heading, the hint bar, the Back button and the touch controls stay put. A scrolling menu has a thin strip (about half a row, never under 10 dp) reserved above and below it, and a small pixel-art chevron centred in the bottom strip says rows are hidden below, with a flipped one in the top strip for rows hidden above; the strips are taken from the pixels whole-row snapping frees where they can be, so the chevron never sits over a row or its cursor, and a strip shrinks before a row is dropped. Neither chevron shows when everything fits, they do not move under reduced motion, and there is no scrollbar. In a scrolling list every row is as tall as the tallest (a Save slot whose text wraps), and never under the density's row height, so a taller row cannot leave a sliver of the next row at an edge; a list that fits is left as it is. The selected row is always kept in view for keys, the D-pad and a gamepad, a drag scrolls and a tap chooses the row under the finger. Built in "Built in menu scrolling" in [MOBILE_PLAN.md](MOBILE_PLAN.md).

## Lifecycle and performance
- Pause when the app goes to the background, when the screen locks and when the Back gesture is used in a level.
- Audio unlocks on the first touch.
- The 60 Hz fixed timestep is kept. Watch battery and heat on long sessions, and cap the render rate if a phone cannot hold it.
- Keep the screen awake while playing (built in slice 7.2; see "Keeping the screen on and auto-pause").
- Frame times (built in slice 6b.1): open the `?debug` page and read `__lf.debugState.perf` (`p50`, `p95`, `p99` in milliseconds, `long` frames over 25 ms, `frames` since the last reset); `__lf.debugPerfReset()` starts a fresh window. See "Trying it on a phone" in `MOBILE_PLAN.md`.

### Fullscreen and the landscape lock (built in slice 7.1)
- **The gesture rule.** Fullscreen is requested, and the landscape lock (`screen.orientation.lock`) after it, only from a tap or key that starts or resumes a run: New game, Continue, a slot on Load game from the title, and Resume. The request is made synchronously inside the gesture, before the Ben-wave delay, because the browser only allows it while the gesture's user activation lasts; a gamepad press grants none, so starting with the pad does not enter fullscreen. Tapping Options or Controls never does. Every failure is silent, the automatic request never leaves fullscreen, and it never asks from an event handler, so there is no loop. (The Fullscreen button, below, is the one thing that calls `exitFullscreen`, and only when pressed.)
- **The button and `F`** (built in slice 7.6) are the explicit way in and out: see "Fullscreen button" below. They work whatever the setting says.
- **The setting** is Auto: on for touch devices, off on desktop. `?fullscreen` (or `=on`) forces it on everywhere and `?fullscreen=off` forces it off; the Options > Display screen sets it (built in slice 7.4; see "Display screen" above). In the Tauri app nothing is requested, and in an installed web app (already fullscreen) only the orientation is locked.
- **Back leaves fullscreen and pauses.** On Android, Back in element fullscreen leaves fullscreen and fires no `popstate`, so the game listens for `fullscreenchange` and pauses a level in play when fullscreen ends; Resume enters fullscreen again with one tap. In an installed app Back should reach `BackGuard` as with `?back` (to verify on a device). `BackGuard` is never armed in the Tauri app, where the native Back calls `Game.back()`.
- **Secure context.** Neither fullscreen nor the lock needs one in the specifications, so both should work over LAN http; the wake lock does need one. Unconfirmed on a device. On an iPhone there is no element fullscreen (`document.fullscreenEnabled` is false) and the request is skipped.

### Fullscreen button (built in slice 7.6)
- **What it is.** A round pixel-art button at the top right (Expand: four corner brackets pointing out; Collapse, while fullscreen: pointing in) named `Fullscreen` or `Exit fullscreen`, and the `F` key on a keyboard. Both enter element fullscreen, or leave it. They are explicit, so they work with Options > Display > Fullscreen Off and under `?fullscreen=off`, which only govern the automatic requests when a run starts or resumes. Auto stays touch-only: a desktop never goes fullscreen by itself.
- **Where.** On a touch screen: the title and the pause menu with every screen opened over them, 48 dp, beside the Pause pill (alone in the corner on the title's top level), and never in play, where the corner is Pause's and the run already went fullscreen on its gesture. On a desktop: the title, the pause menu, level cards and play, small. Nowhere on the scenes, and not in the app or on an iPhone, where element fullscreen cannot work (the same rule as the Display screen's Fullscreen row).
- **`F`.** Free in both keyboard layouts, so nothing was rebound. It acts on the key-down edge only (a held key does not toggle again), ignores Ctrl, Alt and Meta, and never fires while typing in a field. The Controls table lists it and the hint bar ends with `F Fullscreen` where it works (not on the Controls screen, which has the table); the touch Controls table lists the button.
- **Leaving fullscreen in play pauses the level** (slice 7.1), so pressing the button or `F` to leave from a level lands on the pause menu, which is also where Esc leaves you in a browser without the Keyboard Lock (the browser handles it). Entering never pauses.

### Esc and the Pause key in fullscreen
- **Esc.** A desktop browser spends Esc on leaving fullscreen and does not deliver it to the page. Where Chromium's Keyboard Lock exists (secure context, not touch) the game locks Esc once fullscreen starts and unlocks it when it ends, so Esc works as it does outside fullscreen: it pauses in play (the game stays fullscreen) and closes a screen over a menu. On the top level of the pause menu and of the title, Esc then leaves fullscreen and the menu stays open; the pause menu is resumed by its Resume row, `P` or the Pause key. The browser still leaves fullscreen on a held Esc (about two seconds).
- **Without the lock** (Firefox, Safari, plain http on a LAN address): Esc leaves fullscreen through the browser, the level pauses by the auto-pause rule and the pause menu is waiting. Nothing is hidden and the Fullscreen button works as before.
- **The Pause key** (Pause/Break) pauses in play and resumes from the pause menu, in both layouts, in or out of fullscreen. It ignores key repeat and typing in a field. The hint bar and the desktop Controls table list it with Esc and `P`; while Esc leaves fullscreen the hint bar reads `Esc Exit fullscreen` and Resume shows `P Pause`. See `docs/MOBILE_PLAN.md` ("Built in the Esc and Pause keys in fullscreen") for the table of Esc per screen and what is not verified.
- **Not verified on a device:** the request inside a tap, the landscape lock, and the resize that follows.

### Keeping the screen on and auto-pause (built in slice 7.2)
- **Wake lock policy.** The screen is kept on (`navigator.wakeLock.request('screen')`) only while all of these hold: the screen is live (play, a cinematic, dialogue, the ending, credits, a stinger or a level card, with no sub-screen over it), the page is visible, the Rotate screen is not up, there was input within the last five minutes (`IDLE_RELEASE_MS`), the setting is on, and the browser has a wake lock in a secure context. It is let go on the title, the pause menu, any sub-screen, loading, a hidden page, Rotate and after five minutes without a key, pointer, touch or pad input, and asked for again when the conditions return. The same rule applies on desktop, which helps gamepad players. The setting is on by default; the Options > Display screen turns it off (slice 7.4), `?wake=off` forces it off and `?wake` (or `=on`) forces it on.
- **How it behaves.** One request is in flight at a time; if the ask is withdrawn before it resolves the lock is released as soon as it does. The browser drops the lock when the page hides, so a return to visible asks again if the ask still stands. A refusal (for example `NotAllowedError` under battery saver) is not retried until the ask is next raised, so the game never loops on a refusal. It never throws, and `__lf.debugState.lifecycle.wake` reports `{ held, requests, failures, lastError }`; `awake` is what the policy wants, which can be true while `held` is false.
- **Secure context.** The wake lock needs one, so over plain LAN http it is unavailable and the game plays on with no error; `adb reverse tcp:5173 tcp:5173` and `http://localhost:5173/` give a secure context. In the Tauri app the web lock is never built: the policy still runs and drives a native backend the app injects (planned for the app phase).
- **Auto-pause table.** Each event pauses only a level in play (through `autoPause()`, so it can never pause twice or pause a menu), and coming back never resumes.

| Event | Pauses | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Page hidden or `pagehide` | yes | Existing. |
| Return to visible | no | The pause menu stays up. |
| Window `blur` | yes, on all devices | The notification shade or recent-apps gesture on a phone, and alt-tab on desktop, which no longer leaves Ben standing among enemies. Focus returning does not resume. |
| Phone turned upright (Rotate) | yes | Existing. |
| Leaving fullscreen | yes | Slice 7.1; this is Android Back. |
| `gamepaddisconnected` | yes, when the pad was the one in use | The gamepad was the last input device and the unplugged pad was the last one with input. A pad that was not in use does not pause. |
| Audio route change, a call, low battery | no | App phase, if ever. |

- **Not verified:** the wake lock holding the screen on a real phone, the notification shade and recent-apps gesture sending `blur` or hidden, and a real pad unplug. The unit tests use fakes, and the browser check asserts what is wanted, not what the headless browser holds.

## Haptics
Haptics are a third consumer of the events that already drive audio and captions, so they fit without touching the sim. The plugin side is specified in `design/uploads/HAPTICS_PLUGIN_UPGRADE.md`, a proposal for `tauri-plugin-haptics` (branch `chore/integrate-jules-haptics` in `haptics-lab-app`). Lieutenant Fizz is its first game client, and Haptics Lab is where patterns are tuned.

### The plugin ladder
- **Portable patterns.** A pattern is a list of transient and continuous events with intensity and sharpness over time. The plugin compiles it to the best of five tiers the device supports: envelope (API 36), composition (OEM-tuned primitives), amplitude waveform, on/off waveform, then silent. The result reports which tier played.
- **Register once, trigger by id.** Patterns are registered at startup, and in-game triggers are a short id plus a scale, which keeps the invoke round trip small when Ben jumps three times a second.
- **Native policies:** interrupt, queue, drop-if-busy and coalesce, run in Kotlin, so a run of twelve Cheezies does not become twelve cancelled effects.
- **A UI lane** for menus (`plugin:haptics|ui`: confirm, reject, tick, toggle, drag start) uses the system's own feedback constants, which follow the user's touch-feedback setting and ignore the master scale. The backend exposes it as `ui()`, but the game does not call it: menu cues play as ordinary patterns so they follow the game's Strength setting (Off is silent) and feel as present as the gameplay cues, which a player who chose Strong expects. The system's touch-feedback setting is therefore not consulted for menus.
- **Controllers:** with `target: 'auto'`, gameplay patterns go to a connected gamepad's vibrator and the phone stays silent. The UI lane stays on the phone. This is also how desktop **Rumble** works.
- **Master scale and `maxTier`:** a game intensity slider, and a lab override to preview a weaker phone.
- **Fixes the plugin needs first** (section 7 of the proposal): remove the hidden `thud` and `pop` predefined ids, report each primitive's support separately, keep the scale on composition steps, gate the envelope tier on API 36 and its support check, and always return `tier` and `target`.

### The plugin backend
`packages/engine/src/haptic-plugin.ts` implements `HapticBackend` over the plugin's commands (`plugin:haptics|capabilities`, `play`, `stop`, `ui`) through an injected `invoke` (Tauri's own, looked up on first use, so the web bundle never touches it). It reads `capabilities()` once and compiles each `HapticPattern` into one request at the best tier the device and the tier cap allow, stepping down with a reason at each step:
1. **Envelope (tier 4)** for a pattern with a hum, where the device reports envelope support (API 36 and up): control points sampled along the curves, sharpness mapped to a frequency inside the device's profile, taps as short ramps; it steps down if the points or the length pass the device's limits.
2. **Composition (tier 3)** for a pattern of taps only, where the motor has primitives: sharpness picks the primitive (tick from 0.85, click from 0.5, low tick from 0.3, thud below), the strength becomes its scale, gaps become `delayMs`, and a missing primitive swaps for a neighbour the motor has. A tap of 0.7 or more is followed by a thud so the strongest cues land with weight. A hum has no primitive, so a pattern with one skips this tier.
3. **Amplitude waveform (tier 2)** where the motor has amplitude control: one waveform of 5 ms cells holding the strongest amplitude wanted, taps as short bursts shaped like the `vibrate` pulses and hums sampled every 20 ms.
4. **On and off (tier 1)**: the `navigator.vibrate` compiler's array, sent as a one-shot or a waveform that starts with a zero off time.
The strength sent is shaped, not linear: `min(1, 1.3 * strength ^ 0.6)` where strength is the cue's intensity times the master scale, lifted to at least 0.3 for a primitive (0.25 of the range for an amplitude cell). A jump (a tap of 0.5) therefore goes out as a click at 0.86 at Strong, 0.72 at Medium and 0.57 at Light, where it went out at 0.5, 0.38 and 0.25 when the strength was passed straight through and a Pixel 8 Pro barely felt anything below 0.3. The same curve shapes the on and off tier (a tap's strength, and each slice of a hum, are curved before they become pulse lengths), with the strength floor still applied to the raw strength. Every constant (`PLUGIN_COMPILE`: the curve, the gain, the two floors, the sharpness thresholds and the thud threshold) is tunable with `debugHapticsTune({ plugin: { gamma: 0.7 } })` and has a slider under Phone (app plugin) on the lab's Compile tab; Copy as JSON carries what moved. A pattern under the strength floor (0.08) sends nothing. The backend's `play` fires the call and returns an estimate straight away; the estimate carries `compiled` (the effect as on and off times, on first) so `GameHaptics` charges the vibration budget with motor time only, not delays or silent gaps (an envelope control point ramps from the previous amplitude, so a ramp down to zero counts as on); the plugin's own tier, downgrade reason and policy arrive in `lastResult()` and `onResult`, and a rejection is recorded, never thrown. The master strength is the `scale` every `GameHaptics` play already carries; a tier cap (`setMaxTier`, used by the haptics page) is compiled in and sent as `maxTier`. Inside the app, `adoptPlugin` makes it the phone's backend when the plugin reports a vibrator; otherwise `navigator.vibrate` stays. The game does not yet use the plugin's `register` and `trigger` (native policies): `GameHaptics` still runs the priorities, policies and budget in TypeScript. The player app's `haptics.html` plays the real cue table through this backend (see [TAURI_SPIKE.md](TAURI_SPIKE.md)). On a Pixel 8 Pro (Android 17) the plugin compiles and `capabilities()` works, smoke cases 1 to 4 and 7 to 9 behave as expected and tier 1, 2 and 3 cues have been felt; smoke cases 5 and 6 and a real tier 4 envelope are unverified, as they need a device whose motor lacks an effect or a primitive, or has envelope support.

### In the game
- **Mechanism in the engine, content in the episode.** A `GameHaptics` class beside `GameAudio` in `packages/engine` takes a backend, a cue-to-pattern table, the master scale and per-cue cooldowns. The episode supplies the table in `episodes/episode-1/src/haptics/fizz-haptics.ts`, as `audio/patterns.ts` supplies audio patterns, so each episode tunes its own feel. Patterns are authored in Haptics Lab and exported as JSON.
- **Pluggable backends**, so the engine never depends on Tauri: none (the web default), the Tauri plugin (loaded lazily, only inside the app: Episode 1 `import()`s `haptic-plugin` in the app-host branch, so the compiler is its own chunk that the web build never requests, and cues go to `navigator.vibrate` until the plugin has loaded and been adopted), `navigator.vibrate` (durations only) and gamepad rumble through `vibrationActuator`.
- **Hook points in `game.ts`:** in `caption()` next to `audio.caption` for cue effects, in `onEvent` for `DIED`, `LEVEL_COMPLETE` and `BOSS_HP`, and beside the menu and click sounds for the UI lane.
- **Settings:** Options > Haptics (see "Haptics screen" above): Strength for the phone, Rumble for a controller, the lab switch and Reset. They go through the shared options model in APP.md, and the entry is hidden when there is no vibrator or controller.
- **Rules:** fire-and-forget (never awaited in the frame loop), rate-limited, and never feeding back into the sim.

### Web haptics
The first backend is `navigator.vibrate`. Haptics are on by default where a device can use them, at the Strength the player chose (Strong until then): the phone's vibrator is used on a touch device, and a pad's motors while a pad is the device in use. Strength Off, and `?haptics=off`, send nothing to the phone, menu cues included. `?haptics` (or `?haptics=on`) forces haptics on for the visit at the saved strength (Strong when the saved one is Off) and `?haptics=off` forces them off; neither writes anything to storage, and the Haptics screen shows the forced rows as "On (link)" or "Off (link)" and will not change them. A desktop keyboard session leaves the phone's vibrator silent unless `?haptics` asks for it.
- **Who has it.** Chrome for Android, after a tap on the page. Firefox removed the Vibration API in version 129 (desktop and Android), so Firefox for Android cannot be used to feel the haptics; Safari on iOS has never had it. Detection is by type (`typeof navigator.vibrate === 'function'`), and a `false` return is treated as unavailable from then on. Nothing is sent before `navigator.userActivation.hasBeenActive`, and an unsupported browser is a quiet no-op, never an error. The phone's silent or Do Not Disturb modes mute it.
- **Where it fires.** Gameplay cues come from `Game.caption()`, beside the audio, so they work with Captions off. They fire only in a level (`screen === 'play'`) and only while the page is visible, with one exception: the title's attract loop raises captions too, and on the bare title (no Options, Controls or Load screen open) a world cue whose source is on screen, such as the Citadel boulder's KRUNCH, plays at 0.6 of its strength (`ATTRACT_SCALE`) and nothing else from the loop does. Menu cues win a frame against it and cut it off, and it never interrupts one. Coming back to the map from a level does not raise the pogo-off cue, and entering a level plays `levelStart` (a thud, then a click 80 ms later; a calm cue) once. A cue for something out in the level (a thunk, a crumble, a boss slam) is felt only when it is on screen. Menu cues are played as game patterns at 1.5 times the Strength setting (`UI_BOOST`), never through the system's view haptics. Menu cues come from `nav`, `activate`, `closeSub` and the Options steppers (a tick for a move, a firmer one for Select, a softer one for Back, two for a disabled row, and an on or off pattern for toggles). Pogo on and off are read from the `POGO_ON` state each frame, because toggling raises no event; `GAME_OVER` and the boss reaching 0 hit points have their own cues. Hiding the page, leaving the level or disposing the game sends `vibrate(0)`.
- **Who plays.** Cues raised in a frame wait for `flush()`, which sends at most one to the backend. Each cue has a priority (0 to 4), a cooldown and a policy. A cue never cuts off a higher priority that is still running. **Interrupt** replaces what runs; **drop-if-busy** is skipped while anything runs; **queue** waits for the running pattern if it ends within 250 ms and is dropped otherwise; **coalesce** folds the repeats in one frame into a single pulse that is 15% stronger for each extra one (up to three), and ignores repeats inside its window (snacks use 60 ms). Between equal priorities the longer pattern wins the frame. The phone also has a **budget** of 400 ms of vibration in any rolling second (checked before a cue plays and charged after; priority 4 is never held back), which saves the battery and keeps a run of small cues from turning into a buzz. The master strength scales every cue.
- **Double hits.** The sim raises two things for one moment in two places. Level cleared raises the "ta-da!" caption and `LEVEL_COMPLETE`: only the caption has a cue. A boss hit raises "ZZZAP" and `BOSS_HP`: every hit is the ZZZAP cue, and `BOSS_HP` adds the long fade only at 0 hit points (the longer pattern wins the frame). `DIED` has no cue, because "whoa!" is the hurt.
- **Silent on purpose.** The enemy noises (snort, skreee, pfff, zap zap zap, brrrt, splorp), poof and the stage scene's low sting have no pattern, so the phone is not buzzing all the time. They are `null` in the table, and a test checks that every caption the sim can raise (read from the WASM) and every caption the audio knows has either a pattern or an explicit `null`.
- **Calm.** When Motion is Reduced (or the system asks for it), cues marked `calm` (the boss slam, the dome, a game over, a thunk) squeeze any hum into 150 ms and play no stronger than 0.7. Haptics stay on.
- **Compiling a pattern.** `vibrate` takes only on and off times, so strength has to become pulse length. The constants live in `VIBRATE_COMPILE` (`period` 20 ms, `minOn` 6, `minOff` 4, `floor` 0.16, `tBase` 8, `tSpan` 16, `maxMs` 1000). Effective strength is `i = min(1, v × cue scale × master)`, and under the floor nothing plays. A tap is one pulse of `round((8 + 16·i) × (1.15 − 0.3·sharpness))` ms. A hum is cut into 20 ms slices sampled in the middle: at 0.9 or more the slice is fully on, otherwise it is on for `max(6, round(v × 20))` ms of it, and below the floor it is off (sharpness is ignored). Everything goes on one timeline, touching runs merge, gaps under 4 ms are filled, the trailing off is dropped, and the array is clipped at 1000 ms. It starts with an on time (a leading 0 when the pattern starts silent). Worked examples: Jump (0.5 strength, 0.7 sharp) gives `[15]`, and `[11]` at half strength; Fzzt `[12]`; Bonk `[21]`; Whoa (a tap, then a 120 ms hum fading from 0.9) `[59, 6, 11, 9, 8, 12, 6]`.
- **Debugging.** With `?debug`, `__lf.debugHaptics()` returns what each backend can do (`caps.device`, `caps.controller`), where gameplay cues are going (`route`), the last 20 plays with their target and what each compiled to, how many cues were dropped and why (`cooldown`, `busy`, `budget`, `frame`) and the vibration spent in the last second. `__lf.debugHapticsTune(patch)` changes the table while it runs and returns `{ applied, refused }`; for example `{ cues: { jump: { events: [{ kind: 'transient', at: 0, intensity: 0.8, sharpness: 0.5 }] } }, compile: { floor: 0.2 }, budget: { onMs: 300 } }`. A cue edit may change `events`, `priority`, `cooldownMs`, `policy`, `world` and `calm`; every value is range-checked and what fails is refused by name.
- **The Haptics lab.** Options > Haptics > Haptics lab (Off by default; `?debug` shows it too, and `?debug&lab=haptics` opens it) adds the Lab button, shared with the sound lab, whose panel header has a Sound | Haptics switch while both labs can be reached. It has four tabs: Cues (a button per cue in the groups Ben, Pickups, World, Boss, Level, Menus and Editor, each with its compiled length on the phone and the pad), Tune (edit up to six events, the cooldown, priority and policy, with the pulse array and a bar timeline for each target, Play and Play original), Compare (phone then controller three times 400 ms apart, a cue at 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0, and a floor ladder of ten taps from 0.1 to 1.0 to find where taps stop being felt) and Compile (sliders for the vibrate and rumble compilers, the plugin's strength curve and the budget). The header picks Auto, Phone, Controller or Off (a disabled one says why), sets a lab-only strength from 0 to 1.5, copies what moved as JSON in the `debugHapticsTune` shape, resets, closes (Escape does too, without pausing the game) and shows a status line: whether the phone and a pad can play, whether the page has had a tap, and the last cue with what it compiled to. Controls are 48 px tall and the type follows the Fizz sizes; at 740×360 the panel scrolls under its tabs. Auditions play regardless of the Strength setting and the lane, but not while the page is hidden. The lab's code is loaded the first time it is opened.
- **Unverified.** Whether 6 to 12 ms pulses can be felt on a given phone, how soft the pulse-width fades feel, and whether `vibrate` works over plain http on the LAN (use `adb reverse` for a secure context if not) all need a device.

### Controller rumble
With a gamepad, gameplay cues rumble the pad instead of the phone, through `Gamepad.vibrationActuator`.
- **Who has it.** Chrome on the desktop (the Gamepad API needs a secure context there; `localhost` is one), Safari 16.4 and later. Firefox has it only behind a preference and is treated as no rumble. Whether Chrome for Android rumbles a Bluetooth pad is unknown and needs a device. A pad whose actuator is missing is simply unavailable, never an error.
- **Routing.** The input device in use (`InputManager.device`) picks where gameplay cues go: a gamepad means the controller, touch or keyboard means the phone's vibrator. If the controller cannot rumble, gameplay cues are dropped; they do not fall back to the phone, which is not in the player's hands. Menu cues stay on the phone's vibrator whatever the device. The Rumble level (Options > Haptics) scales the controller; the phone's Strength does not. `InputManager.activePad()` returns the pad that last had input (or the first connected one) for the rumble backend. A change of route stops whatever was running.
- **Compiling a pattern.** A pad has two motors, a heavy low one and a light high one, and takes steady effects: each `playEffect` replaces the one running. The tunable constants live in `RUMBLE_COMPILE`. Strength `i = min(1, v × cue scale × master)` splits by sharpness: the low motor gets `i × (1 − sharpness)` and the high one `i × sharpness`, so a dull thud shakes the grips and a click buzzes. A tap is one segment of `40 + 40 × i` ms (a motor needs time to spin up): Bonk gives one 68 ms segment at 0.56 low and 0.14 high. A hum is cut into slices of at least 40 ms sampled in the middle, and neighbours within 0.05 in both motors merge. Where segments overlap the later one takes over. A pattern is at most 8 segments and 1000 ms. Each segment is one `playEffect`, fired by a timer at its start; a new play, `stop()`, hiding the page or leaving the level cancels the timers and calls `reset()`. A rejected effect (a hidden page, an unplugged pad) is swallowed.
- **Budget and strength.** The 400 ms-per-second budget is for the phone's battery and does not apply to a controller. The two targets keep their own strength, running pattern and queue, and each gets at most one call a frame.
- **Unverified.** How the cues feel on a real pad (the 40 ms minimum is a guess at hiding Bluetooth jitter), the rumble cap on Chrome (about 5 s, beyond our 1 s), and a pad on Chrome for Android.

### The Fizz patterns
The proposal's section 8 gives each event a pattern, the effect at tiers 4, 3 and 2, and a policy. In short:

| Moment | Policy | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Jump | interrupt | a light tap |
| High pogo | interrupt | a short rising ramp |
| Pogo on and off | UI lane toggle | two taps on, one off |
| Fizz fired | drop-if-busy | a sharp, light tap |
| Out of fizz | interrupt | two soft low ticks |
| Stomp | interrupt | a thump |
| Stun (bubble hit) | drop-if-busy | a crisp tap |
