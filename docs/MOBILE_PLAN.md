# Mobile implementation plan

**Status: in progress (slices 0 to 4 built, slice 5 built: 5a, 5b and 5c, slice 6 built: 6.1, 6.2 and 6.3, then the split menus: 6.4).** This is the build plan for [MOBILE.md](MOBILE.md): what to change in the code, in what order, how each step is tested and what "done" means. It is web-first. Touch, the phone layout and basic haptics extend the game as it is today and ship on the website, with no Tauri and no new app. [APP.md](APP.md) (the launcher and the Tauri app) comes after, and reuses everything here.

## Principles
- **Playable on a phone as early as possible.** The first two slices are enough to play Crater Fields with two thumbs.
- **Pure logic, thin DOM.** Touch geometry, bit mapping, hint choice and the haptics cue table are pure functions with unit tests, following the repo's convention (no DOM in vitest). The DOM layers only wire events to them.
- **Engine owns the mechanism, the episode owns the content.** The touch controller, the haptics runtime and the input plumbing live in `packages/engine`. The look of the controls, the cue table and the copy live in `episodes/episode-1`.
- **No new rules in the sim.** Touch and haptics are presentation. The one sim touch is reading state the shell may not see yet (pogo on or off), and that is a read-only export.
- **Docs lead, code follows.** Each slice updates MOBILE.md and STATUS.md in the same pull request.
- **Each slice ends with `bun run validate` green** and the touch e2e added in slice 2 passing.

## What the code does today
Findings from reading the current code, with the seams each slice uses.

**Input** (`packages/engine/src/input.ts`)
- Held bits are LEFT 1, RIGHT 2, UP 4, DOWN 8, JUMP 16, POGO 32, FIRE 64, CONFIRM 128, mirrored in `episodes/episode-1/src/sim/protocol.ts` and the Rust world. There is no menu bit: pause, confirm, quick save and zoom are `Command`s emitted from the private `onKeyDown`.
- `InputManager.peek()` ORs keyboard bits and gamepad bits and is called once per frame (menus) and again by `poll()` in each fixed step. The shell passes **held** bits and the sim does its own edge detection, so a tap shorter than one 1/60 s step would be lost. Touch needs a latch (as `confirmPending` already does for Enter).
- `InputDevice` is `'keyboard' | 'gamepad'` with a pure, tested `nextDevice`. Touch is a third value.
- Menus read the same bits: UP and DOWN edges move, JUMP or FIRE edges activate. A touch JUMP button that fed the menus would activate the highlighted row, so touch bits must be ignored outside play. The `blocked` flag in `InputManager` looks like the intended hook and is unused.
- Pogo is a **toggle** on the POGO edge, so its button is a tap that raises the bit for at least one tick. Jump height depends on JUMP staying **held**. UP with FIRE aims up. DOWN aims down only in the air or on a ladder, and looks down on the ground.

**Overlay and CSS** (`episodes/episode-1/src/ui.ts`, `ui.css`, `index.html`)
- `#stage` holds `#sky`, `#gl`, `#fx` and `#ui` (`pointer-events: none`). Menus, cards, the letterbox, dialogue, credits and the stinger turn `pointer-events` on.
- Every pointer handler is a `click`, plus `mouseenter` for hover-select. There is no pointer handling in play. An Options row only cycles forward on a click.
- There is no `viewport-fit=cover`, no `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, no `touch-action`, no `user-select: none`, no tap-highlight rule, and `100vh` is used where `100dvh` is needed. `#hud` and `#panelBtn` sit at a fixed 16 px.
- `layout.ts` computes the `--lf-*` variables from `window.innerWidth` and `innerHeight` and listens to `resize` only (no `orientationchange` or `visualViewport`). At 844×390 and 740×360 the type scale is n = 2, so rows are about 26 px tall and need padding to reach 48 dp.
- The hint bar is built from the pure `hints.ts` (`HintContext { device, layout }`). The Controls screen has three fixed columns and the pause menu shows F5 and F9, which mean nothing on touch.

**Canvas** (`packages/engine/src/renderer.ts`, `Game.frame`)
- The pixel ratio is capped at 2. A phone at DPR 2.6 to 3.5 therefore renders at 2× and is stretched by the browser, so sprite pixels have uneven widths. The camera shows a fixed 13 tiles of height and derives the horizontal span from the aspect, so tile size is `height / 13`, not a whole number of device pixels.
- The URL bar changes `innerHeight` and with it the zoom. On a 390 dp tall phone a sprite pixel is about 1.9 CSS pixels.

**Flow** (`episodes/episode-1/src/game.ts`)
- The `pause` command, `onVisibility` (auto-pause and audio suspend on `visibilitychange` and `pagehide`), the audio unlock on the first `pointerdown` and `keydown`, and `debugShow` already exist. Window `blur` only clears keys, and there is no wake lock, fullscreen, orientation or manifest anywhere.
- Cinematic, dialogue, ending, credits and stinger advance on JUMP or FIRE edges, Enter, or their on-screen Continue and Skip buttons. There is no tap-to-advance on the text area.

**Options** (`options.ts`)
- Key `lf-ep1-options-v1`. `parseOptions` fills missing fields from defaults, so adding a field needs no version bump. A new row needs: the `Options` field, `DEFAULT_OPTIONS`, `parseOptions`, a `CHOICES` entry in `stepOption`, a row in `OPTION_ROWS`, a value-text branch in `optionItems()` (it silently falls through to the Motion text for an unknown key) and an `applySettings` case.

**Events for haptics**
- The sim raises `Ev.CAPTION` with a caption id, and `Game.caption()` calls `audio.caption(text)`. That call is the hook for cue haptics. The ids are the `Cap` enum in `episodes/episode-1/game/src/text.rs`. The ones that matter: Jump, Boing (pogo bounce), Kick (wall-kick), Fzzt (fizz shot), NoFizz, Bonk (stomp stun), Sproing and Bwomp (marshmallow), Whoa (hurt), Crunch (snack), ExtraLife, the gumdrop and USB pickups, TaDa (level win), Zzzap (boss hit), Thoom (boss slam), Clang (dome open), Fizzled and Poof (enemy gone). Menu feedback is `audio.play('menu')` and `audio.play('click')`.

## Architecture
### Touch input (engine)
- **`packages/engine/src/touch.ts`** (pure, tested; built in slice 1, with no UI): the geometry and the held state.
  - `TouchLayout` describes the control rectangles in CSS pixels: the D-pad circle, Jump, Pogo, Fizz and Pause, each with a visual size and a hit area of at least 48 dp.
  - `dpadDirections(dx, dy, radius)` turns a thumb's offset from the D-pad centre into left, right, up and down. Inside a dead zone (0.3 of the radius) nothing is held. A diagonal holds two directions, unless one axis is more than twice the other, when only the stronger counts, so running right with the thumb drifting a little down never aims or looks down. Positions outside the circle count like the edge, so a sliding thumb stays captured.
  - `hitTest(layout, x, y)` finds the control a new touch lands on (the buttons win where hit areas overlap), and `undersizedTargets(layout)` lists controls under 48 dp, for the slice 2 e2e audit.
  - `TouchState` tracks every finger by pointer id (multi-touch: moving and jumping together). `down`, `move`, `up` and `cancelAll` update it and `held(now)` reports what is held. A finger on the D-pad re-evaluates as it slides, so a thumb can roll from left to down without lifting.
  - A freshly pressed Jump, Pogo or Fizz button is held for at least 50 ms (`MIN_HOLD_MS`, about three frames) so a quick tap survives until the next fixed step, while a held button stays held (jump height and pogo height depend on it). Pause is a command, not a bit: `down` reports it and the caller runs the `pause` command.
- **`InputManager`** gains a `touch` source (`TouchState`): the touch bits are combined with keyboard and pad bits by the pure `inputBits`, and count only while `setTouchEnabled(true)` (since slice 3 the game turns them on whenever the controls show, so they drive the menus too; turning them off drops any held finger). `'touch'` joins `InputDevice` and `nextDevice` (a touch makes touch the device, any key or pad input takes it back, and unplugging a pad leaves touch alone), and there is a public `command(cmd)` so the on-screen Pause can emit `pause`, a `noteTouch()` for the DOM controller, and `releaseTouch()`, called on `blur`, `pagehide` and `visibilitychange`. The unused `blocked` flag still silences every source.
- **The DOM controller** (`packages/engine/src/touch-ui.ts`) owns a `#touch` layer between `#fx` and `#ui` (`touch-action: none`), tracks pointers by `pointerId` with `setPointerCapture`, and feeds the pure functions. Buttons are real `<button>` elements with names, so menus and the pause button stay reachable by TalkBack.

### The look (episode)
- The glass controls from design 2a, set in Fizz: a round D-pad with arrow glyphs, Jump (largest, where the thumb rests), Pogo (small, lit while pogo is on) and Fizz (shows the ammo count), a top-left HUD of three glass pills (lives, snacks in yellow, fizz in cyan) and a top-right pause button. Styling is CSS in `ui.css` driven by `--lf-touch-opacity`, and the size, hand and moved controls come from the `TouchSpec` that `touchSpec(settings)` builds (slice 6).
- Pogo's lit state needs the pogo flag. The sim does not export it today (`State.POGO_HEIGHT` is a tuning value, not the toggle), so slice 2 adds one read-only value, `State.POGO_ON`, to the per-frame state in the Rust crate and `protocol.ts`. It is a read-only export and changes no sim behaviour.

### Layout and display
- `index.html`: `viewport-fit=cover`, `user-scalable=no`, `interactive-widget=resizes-content`, a theme colour.
- CSS: `touch-action: manipulation` on every control, `user-select: none`, `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent`, `overscroll-behavior: none`, a `contextmenu` guard, `100dvh`.
- **Safe areas, with the game drawn under them.** The canvas stays full-bleed, so the game renders under the cutout and the system bars. Only the controls, the HUD, menus and hints are kept out of that space, using the CSS `env(safe-area-inset-top|right|bottom|left)` values with `viewport-fit=cover`. The same CSS works in Firefox, Chrome and Safari and in the Tauri webview (Threshold's `MobileToolbar` pads with `env(safe-area-inset-top)` for the same reason), so one set of rules serves the web and the app. `ui.css` defines `--lf-safe-*` from those `env()` values (with a `0` fallback, so tests can override them), `layout.ts` listens to `visualViewport` and `orientationchange` and sizes the type from the visual viewport, and the HUD, controls and menus add the insets to their offsets. The Engine button is hidden on touch. The hint bar is hidden in play on touch and shows touch hints in menus.
- **Portrait** shows a "Rotate your phone" screen while touch is the device, and the game stays paused.

### Pixel-perfect scale on phones (built in slice 4)
- **The rule (Rule A, no bars).** Under Sharp the scale is `s = max(2, floor(H / (16 × target)))` for a canvas `H` device pixels tall, and the view shows `H / (16 × s)` tiles. The targets are 13 tiles in a level, 12 on the map and 14 in a cinematic. The leftover height shows more of the level instead of black bars, so the canvas always fills the screen. The tile count lands between the target and `target × (s + 1) / s` (at most 19.5 at `s = 2`, below the shortest level's 22 tiles). Below 11 tiles (`s = 2` and `H` under 352) the view falls back to Soft. Width is never clamped, so an ultrawide shows more across.
- **When it applies.** Sharp turns on at boot from a coarse pointer, `?touch` or `?pixels=sharp` and stays for the session; `?pixels=soft` forces Soft. Desktop stays Soft (13 tiles) by default. Under Sharp any zoom other than 1 falls back to Soft.
- **The canvas.** The renderer no longer asks Three.js for a pixel ratio. Soft backs the canvas with `floor(css × min(dpr, 2, √(budget / cssArea)))`. Sharp reads `devicePixelContentBoxSize` (falling back to `round(css × dpr)` where it is missing, as on Safari, or where it disagrees with the CSS size) and divides by the smallest whole `k ≥ ceil(dpr / 3)` that fits the budget, so the browser's pixelated upscale is a whole `k`×. If the device size is not divisible by `k` the canvas is Soft. The canvas follows device ratio changes (browser zoom, moving monitors).
- **Pixel budget.** `PIXEL_BUDGET = 3840 × 2160` caps a canvas. Everything up to 4K at ratio 1, 1.5 and 2, and 3440×1440, is unchanged; 5K, 6K and 8K are capped.
- **Examples (Sharp).**

| Screen (device px) | Scale | Tiles tall |
|---|---|---|
| 844×390 at 3× (2532×1170) | 5 | 14.625 |
| 740×360 at 2.6× (1924×936) | 4 | 14.625 |
| 1366×768 | 3 | 16 |
| 1920×1080 | 5 | 13.5 |
| 1920×1200 | 5 | 15 |
| 2560×1440 | 6 | 15 |
| 3440×1440 | 6 | 15 (about 36 across) |
| 3840×2160 | 10 | 13.5 |
| a 600 px tall window | 2 | 18.75 |
| 5K at 2× (5120×2880, `k = 2`) | 6 | 15 |

- **Checking it.** `?debug` exposes `debugView` (`sharp`, `scale`, `tiles`, `k`, `budgeted`, `dpr`, device, canvas and CSS sizes) and `debugPixels`, and the engine panel shows the pixel scale next to the tile count. `e2e/touch-scale.spec.ts` measures the sprite pixel on both phone projects (and with `?pixels=soft` to show the measure can fail), and `e2e/scale.spec.ts` proves the desktop is unchanged and checks a 3840×2160 canvas.
- **Risks.** The URL bar or fullscreen can step `s` (a visible zoom jump); the page never scrolls, so it is rare, and slice 7's fullscreen changes the height once. The view grows from 13 to 14.6 tiles on the phones (about 12%), and short windows can show up to 19.5 tiles; a playtest of every level on a phone is part of acceptance. Integrated GPUs at 4K and 120 to 144 Hz are protected by the budget; a frame-rate cap is a slice 9 option.

### Menus on the controls
After the first phone test the controls are the primary way through menus, so the thumbs never leave them. Taps stay as the backup.
- **The controls stay up on menu-style screens.** `Game.menuInput` already reads input bits (Up and Down edges move, Left and Right adjust, Jump or Fizz choose, Pogo goes back), so the touch bits can drive it exactly as a gamepad does. Touch bits are no longer gated to play: they count whenever the touch layer is visible, and the layer shows on every screen except loading and the Rotate screen.
- **A reduced set, relabelled.** In menus the D-pad stays; Jump is relabelled "Select" and Pogo "Back" (the faces change with the screen); Fizz is hidden, since it duplicates Select; Pause keeps its job (open the pause menu, close it, or, from a screen opened over a menu, leave the whole menu; see "Built for the Pause and Back buttons"; Back closes one level). The labels come from the hint model, so they read the same as the hint bar.
- **Select and Back act on lift.** In play the buttons act on the press, because a jump cannot wait. On a menu Select and Back (and Pause) act when the finger lifts inside the button, so sliding off cancels the press; the D-pad still acts on the press so it can repeat. A press that began in play and is still down when a menu appears chooses nothing.
- **No accidental choices.** A press only counts as a fresh edge. A finger already holding Jump when a level ends does not choose "Back to the map", because the card appears while Jump is down and `lastBits` already has it. Hiding the layer still drops every finger.
- **Auto-repeat in menus.** A held Up, Down, Left or Right repeats after about 350 ms and then every 90 ms, on touch and on the keyboard, so long lists (save slots, options) and sliders are quick. It is a pure helper with unit tests (a timer function of time held), applied inside `menuInput`.
- **Touch hints.** `InputDevice` `'touch'` gets its own hint text in `hints.ts`: "D-pad Choose · Select · Back", with no Esc, F5 or F9. The pause menu shows the slot, not the keyboard shortcut.
- **Room for the D-pad.** On touch, menu and card content starts right of the D-pad (about 190 px from the left safe edge), so no row or hint sits under the thumb. Text wraps a little narrower.
- **Taps still work.** One tap on a row chooses it (`pointerup`, `pointerType` checked; the mouse hover path stays for a mouse), rows get a minimum 48 dp hit height through padding, Option rows get ◄ and ► steppers (each 48 dp), and the cinematic, dialogue, ending and card text advance on a tap. This is for TalkBack and for people who prefer touching.
- **Back.** Pogo is Back on every sub-screen. The Back button is built in slice 5b; the browser Back button handling is slice 5c (see "Built in slice 5c").

### Lifecycle (web)
- Fullscreen and the landscape lock are requested on the gesture that starts or resumes a run (built in 7.1; see "Built in slice 7.1"), and an explicit Fullscreen button and `F` key enter and leave it on demand (built in 7.6; see "Built in slice 7.6"). The screen wake lock is held while playing or watching and released on pause, hide, Rotate and after five minutes without input (built in 7.2; see "Built in slice 7.2"). The wake lock needs a secure context; everything degrades silently.
- Auto-pause on `visibilitychange`, `pagehide`, `blur` while playing, a switch to portrait, and `popstate`.
- A `manifest.webmanifest` (`display: fullscreen`, `orientation: landscape`) so "Add to Home screen" gives a clean full-screen launch on Android. It ships in this slice with icons exported at the manifest sizes straight from the chosen 2g design SVG (metadata stripped); built in 7.3 (see "Built in slice 7.3"). The full icon set under `assets/icon/` follows with the app work. No service worker is needed yet.

### Haptics (web first)
- **`packages/engine/src/haptics.ts`**: `GameHaptics` with a backend interface, a cue-to-pattern lookup supplied by the episode, a master scale and per-cue cooldowns (the 180 ms caption throttle is the model). Backends: none, `navigator.vibrate` (durations only, Android Chrome, after a user gesture) and, later, the Tauri plugin and gamepad rumble.
- **`episodes/episode-1/src/haptics/fizz-haptics.ts`**: the cue table, keyed by caption id, with the portable pattern shape from `design/uploads/HAPTICS_PLUGIN_UPGRADE.md` (events with intensity and sharpness) so the same table works later on the plugin. For `navigator.vibrate` each pattern compiles to a short duration list.
- **Hooks:** in `Game.caption()` beside `audio.caption`, for `DIED`, `LEVEL_COMPLETE` and `BOSS_HP` in `onEvent`, and beside `audio.play('menu')` and `'click'` for the UI lane. All are fire-and-forget, `vibrate(0)` on pause and hide, and nothing feeds back into the sim.
- **Initial mapping** (the cue ids above): Jump light tick, Boing thud scaled by the bounce, Kick a tap, Fzzt a light click, NoFizz two soft ticks, Bonk a thump, Sproing and Bwomp a springy tap, Whoa then the death a hit with a fading hum, Crunch a tiny tick (coalesced in runs), ExtraLife a swell, TaDa a rise, Zzzap a heavy hit, Thoom a low rumble, Clang a crisp tap. Menu move a tick, choose a click.

### Settings
- **Touch controls:** Size (S, M, L), Opacity (40, 60, 85 or 100 percent, 85 by default) and Left-handed, plus a drag-to-move editor, with Reset. They are device-local, so they live in their own key (`lf-touch-v1`, `{ v: 1, size, opacity, leftHanded, hapticStrength, pos }`) with the same parse-and-default approach as options, and move into the shared options model with the launcher work in APP.md. `hapticStrength` (0 Off to 3 Strong, Strong by default; a save from before it with `haptics: false` reads as Off) is set on Options > Haptics, which slice 8 H4 added; Rumble and the haptics lab switch are in `lf-ep1-options-v1`.
- A Touch controls row in Options (shown on touch devices) opens the Touch controls screen from the design; Options is reachable from the title and from the pause menu. Sub-screens are a stack, so the editor returns to Touch controls and that returns to Options on the row that opened it.
- **Built in slice 6.3:** the Move controls row opens the editor (`sub = 'touchEdit'`), with Reset for the positions only. The settings are complete for the web; a title-layout row, display settings (6b) and the Audio setting (slice 8b) are the next additions, and each is an additive field or a sibling key, as described in "Built in slice 6.3".

## Trying it on a phone
The game has on-screen controls in a level (the D-pad, Jump, Pogo and Fizz buttons and a Pause button, and the glass HUD pills) and, since slice 3, on every menu-style screen, where they work like a gamepad: the D-pad moves (and repeats when held), Select chooses, Back leaves Options, saves and Controls, and Pause resumes or skips. `?touch` pins the controls on, so `?debug&touch&level=0` shows them on a desktop browser too (a mouse drives them).
- **Same Wi-Fi:** `bun run dev:phone` builds the WASM, starts Vite on every interface and prints the addresses. Open `http://<your-computer>:5173/` for the title screen, or `http://<your-computer>:5173/?debug&level=0` to start straight in Crater Fields (`level` is the level id, so `level=1` is Crystal Caves).
- **A secure context:** plain http over the LAN is not one, so the wake lock and the Gamepad API are unavailable (the game plays on without them and shows no error; for the wake lock use `adb reverse` and `http://localhost:5173/?debug`). Fullscreen and the orientation lock need no secure context in the specifications, so they should work over LAN http (to be confirmed on a device, which is what the slice 7.1 checklist below is for). With the phone plugged in and USB debugging on, `adb reverse tcp:5173 tcp:5173` makes the phone see `http://localhost:5173/`, which is a secure context.
- **A preview without the dev server:** the GitHub Pages build of a branch works for anything that does not need the dev server.
- **Listening to the sound field:** open `http://<your-computer>:5173/?debug&level=0` (or the `adb reverse` address) on headphones, tap once to unlock audio, then jump, shoot and walk towards enemies and pickups on either side of the screen, and compare with `&audio=classic`. In the console of a `?debug` page, `__lf.debugAudio('classic')` and `__lf.debugAudio('enhanced')` switch live and `__lf.debugAudio()` reports the mode, the backend, the context state and how many sounds were placed. Phone speakers are often mono in landscape, so use headphones or a stereo pair to hear the placement.
- **Checking on a phone:** that Ben moves, jumps and pogoes; that Fizz fires and its count drops; that Pause opens the pause menu; that the controls stay out of the cutout and gesture margins while the game draws under them (and whether Firefox honours `viewport-fit=cover`); that portrait shows "Rotate your phone"; that a Bluetooth key or gamepad input hides the controls and a touch brings them back; and the frame rate and heat.
- **Checking the menus (slice 3):** that the pause menu, the level-cleared card, the title, Options and the save slots are driven by the D-pad and Select without lifting a thumb; that a held D-pad direction repeats at a comfortable pace; that Jump held as a level ends does not choose "Back to the map" until it is pressed again; that one tap on a row chooses it and the ◄ ► steppers work; that a tap on the cinematic or dialogue text advances it; that nothing on a menu sits under a thumb; and how the shorter rows feel on a 360 to 390 dp tall phone.
- **Checking Controls and Back (slice 5b):** open Controls from the title and check the Action and Touch table (D-pad, Jump, Pogo, Fizz, Pause, Menus, Save / Load) has no key or gamepad glyph and nothing under a thumb; open Options and Saves from the title and from the pause menu and close each with the "← Back" button at the top left (clear of a notch and of the heading); and check Pause is no longer on those screens.
- **Checking fullscreen and the lock (slice 7.1):** Firefox for Android first, then Chrome, on `http://<your-computer>:5173/?debug` over the LAN (no `adb reverse` needed). The setting is Auto, which is on for a touch device; `&fullscreen=off` turns it off and `&fullscreen` (or `=on`) forces it on, desktop too. (1) Tap New game: the page should go fullscreen, the URL bar should be gone and the game should stay landscape. Hold the phone upright: Chrome stays locked, Firefox 144 and later locks or shows the Rotate screen, and earlier Firefox always rejects the lock. (2) In a level press Android Back: the game should leave fullscreen and show the pause menu, the page should not navigate and `__lf.debugState.back.armed` should be false. - **Checking the wake lock and auto-pause (slice 7.2):** open `http://localhost:5173/?debug` over `adb reverse tcp:5173 tcp:5173` (the wake lock needs a secure context; over LAN http expect `wake.requests` to stay 0 and no errors). Set the phone's screen timeout to 30 seconds. (5) Start a level and leave the phone untouched for 60 seconds: the screen should stay on, and `__lf.debugState.lifecycle` should show `awake: true` and `wake.held: true`. Open the pause menu: the screen should dim at the 30 second timeout and `awake` should be false. Resume, press the power button or switch apps, and return: you should land on the pause menu, and `wake.requests` should rise only once you are back in play. After five minutes of no input in play the screen may dim again (`awake` false until the next touch, key or pad press). (8) Pull the notification shade, or use the recent-apps gesture, during play: the game should pause (window `blur` or hidden). On a desktop, alt-tab away from a level: it should pause too. ## How to listen and tune the sound
Use headphones: phone speakers are often mono in landscape, so the field barely shows there.
- **Music field:** open `/?debug&level=0` (the title screen and every level have music), tap once to unlock audio and listen to a level track such as Crater Fields. The lead should sit slightly left, the bells, hats and counter-melodies slightly right, bass, kick and snare dead centre, and the arpeggio in a tower level should flicker between the ears. In the console, `__lf.debugAudio('classic')` and `__lf.debugAudio('enhanced')` switch live; the music restarts on the new route. Compare against `&audio=classic`.
- **What to check:** a centred part (bass, kick) should be as loud in Enhanced as in Classic; a panned part leaning 0.3 has about 0.7 on one side and 1.3 on the other, with the same total power. If the music feels louder or quieter in Enhanced, say so with the track name.
- **Mastering:** the chain's trim is set so that Enhanced is not louder than Classic, but that is a measurement on a test chord, not on the music. Play the same level (for example Crater Fields) with `?audio=classic` and without, at the same device volume, and switch live with `__lf.debugAudio('classic')` and `__lf.debugAudio('enhanced')`. The chain is flat by default (EQ gains 0 dB, a light compressor, trim 0.8), measured within 0.1 dB of Classic on the game's effects and music and 0.03 dB on noise from 1 to 20 kHz. If Enhanced is louder, lower the trim; if quieter, raise it: `__lf.debugAudioTune({ master: { trim: 0.7 } })` (it starts at 0.8). Then tune the rest the same way: `{ master: { reverb: { sfxSend: 0.05, musicSend: 0.03 } } }` for less room, `{ master: { comp: { ratio: 2 } } }` for a lighter compressor, `{ master: { lowShelf: { gain: 1.5 }, highShelf: { gain: 1 } } }` to hear a little EQ (all three bands start at 0 dB), `{ field: { width: 0.4 } }` for narrower sound effects and `{ partPan: { bell: 0.2 } }` for the music. `__lf.debugAudioTune` returns what it applied and what it refused. Changes last until you reload; write the ones you like back into `MASTER`, `FIELD` or `PART_PAN`.
- **Mix stage (muffle and ducking):** open `/?debug&level=0` (Enhanced is the default), tap once to unlock audio and let the Crater Fields track play. Press Esc (or the Pause button): the music should dull to a muffled, quieter version within a fifth of a second and keep playing; open Options from the pause menu and it stays muffled; resume and it opens out over a third of a second. Talk to a character (dialogue) and the music should sit about 3 dB lower without being muffled; the level-cleared card is lightly muffled. Compare with `__lf.debugAudio('classic')` (pause should leave the music at full level, as before). Tune by ear: `__lf.debugAudioTune({ mix: { pause: { lpf: 700, gain: 0.6 } } })` (the screen showing takes it at once), `{ mix: { pauseCoarse: { lpf: 1800 } } }` for a phone, `{ mix: { dialogue: { gain: 0.6 }, cine: { gain: 0.8 }, card: { lpf: 3000 } } }`. `__lf.debugAudio().mix` reports the shape in force. On a phone, check that the muffle is still audible on the speaker (900 Hz is nearly silent there, hence 1400 Hz on a coarse pointer), and hide and return to the tab while playing: the pause menu should already be muffled. Changes last until you reload; write the ones you like into `MIX`. Move the Music volume in Options: it should change smoothly with no restart of the tune in Enhanced.
- **Rooms (8c.3):** open `/?debug&level=0` (Crater Fields, `outdoor`: close and nearly dry), play for a moment, pause and go back to the map with its menu (`map`: also dry, a little darker), then open `/?debug&level=1` (Crystal Caves, `cave`: a long dark tail on the jump, the fizz and the crunch). Going between levels and the map should be a smooth 0.8 s change of the tail, not a click or a jump in level. Other levels worth hearing: `level=8` (Mirror Shafts, bright), `level=2` (the Citadel, a low ring), `level=12` (the Foundry, a metallic ring), `level=4` (Zarg Lookout, a short high ring) and `level=6` (the Playhouse). `__lf.debugAudio().room` names the room; `?audio=classic` has none and is the dry reference. To retune a room by ear, for example `__lf.debugAudioTune({ rooms: { cave: { sfxSend: 0.2 } } })` (a send of 0.36 puts the cave's reverb about 4 dB under the dry effects, 1 is very wet), `__lf.debugAudioTune({ rooms: { cave: { seconds: 2.4, damping: 0.4 } } })` or `__lf.debugAudioTune({ rooms: { foundry: { ring: { amount: 0.2 } } } })`; copy the numbers you like into `ROOMS` in `episodes/episode-1/src/audio/rooms.ts`. Phones use shorter rooms and 0.8 times the sends. The sends are on a scale where the cave at 0.36 is clearly audible and 1 is a very wet room; they were 8 times lower, which measured 26 dB under the dry sound for the cave and could not be heard.
- **Options > Sound (8c.1):** open `/?debug` (or `/?debug&touch&title=split` on a phone), then Options, then Sound (`__lf.debugShow('sound')` goes straight there). Style starts on Auto and shows the default, Enhanced. Step it to Classic and listen: a boing then a plink should come about a quarter of a second later, apart in the stereo field in Enhanced and both in the middle in Classic. Step Music with a level playing: in Enhanced the level changes at once without the loop restarting, in Classic the loop restarts. Step Effects and listen for the crunch at the new level (silent at zero). Open it from the pause menu and check the music is not muffled there. Sound lab starts Off: set it On and the Lab button appears at once on `/` without `?debug` (beside Pause on a phone), open it, then set it Off and the lab closes and the button goes; the choice survives a reload. Reset takes two taps; it should put Style back on Auto, both meters full and the Sound lab Off and leave Captions, Controls and the rest alone. `/?audio=classic` shows "Classic (link)" and cannot be stepped, and does not change the saved choice. `__lf.debugAudio()` reports the mode. Not verified: how the previews sound (the pan, the 160 ms gap and the 250 ms wait are starting values) or any of it on a real phone.
- **Tuning with the sound lab (8c.8):** open `/?debug&lab` (or `/?debug` and tap the Lab button, which sits beside the Pause button on a phone and beside Engine on a desktop), tap once to start audio, and work through the tabs. *Sounds:* set Pan and Distance, then tap an effect to hear it placed. *Music:* tap a track to play it, tap again to stop, 'Follow the game' returns control. *Rooms and mix:* pick a room or a mix state to hold it (the game keeps it until you choose 'Follow level' or 'Follow screen'), then move its sliders; Pause and Options are the best place to judge a mix state. *Master:* trim, compressor, limiter, EQ, the effect field and the music pans. Toggle Classic and Enhanced at the top to compare at the same volume. When it sounds right, tap 'Copy as JSON' (it lists only the values you moved) and paste the result to the developer, or into `__lf.debugAudioTune(...)`; 'Reset to defaults' puts everything back. Changes last until you reload and nothing is saved to Options. The lab proves nothing about quality; it only removes the console from the loop. Unverified: the clipboard button on a real phone over plain HTTP (browsers may refuse it outside a secure context, in which case the JSON is logged to the console), and the touch layout on a real device (checked at 844x390 in a desktop browser only).
- **Measured offline (Chromium, `OfflineAudioContext`, silent) against what is by ear:** *Measured:* the frequency response of the sound-effect path, the music path and the bare master against a straight pass (within 0.03 dB from 1 to 20 kHz at 24, 32, 44.1 and 48 kHz, down to 0.2 dB at 100 Hz); the level (RMS and peak) and the spectrum of 28 effects and 11 tracks through the chain against Classic (RMS within 0.1 dB on average, extremes -0.4 and +0.7 dB in the neutral room; a cave adds 2.3 dB to effects because the reverb is added); the open music low-pass; the energy of each room's reverb against the dry sound for a click, as a function of send, length, damping and coarse length (see the ENGINE_SPEC Rooms entry). *By ear, and not verified:* whether Enhanced now sounds the same as Classic on a phone or Bluetooth headphones (Firefox's compressor and a Bluetooth codec's own low-pass are outside the measurement; Safari was not measured), whether the cave is wet enough or too wet, whether the room table suits each track, and whether the music's wet level sits right under the effects'. Phone speakers and Bluetooth headphones are not covered.
- **What automated tests cannot prove:** that it sounds better. They check the roles, the pan at every note, the graph and the gain, not the taste.
- **What rooms cannot prove automatically:** how any room sounds (the numbers are first guesses), whether the crossfade is free of clicks when the sends and filters glide at the same time (the nodes and automation are checked, not the audio; an `OfflineAudioContext` render would be needed, and not in CI), how it sounds on a phone speaker (reverb on a small mono speaker mostly muddies, which is why phones get shorter rooms and lower sends), and whether the bigger rooms are louder than the small ones. The convolver normalises its response, so length alone should not change the level, but the higher sends of a cave add a little (about half a decibel by estimate), so match levels by ear and adjust `master.trim` or the sends.

## How to feel the haptics
Haptics need Chrome for Android: Firefox removed the Vibration API in 129, and iOS has none.
- **Open `/`** (haptics are on by default on a phone; add `?debug&level=0` to start in Crater Fields and to get `__lf.debugHaptics()` in the console). Tap the page once first; Chrome refuses `vibrate` until the page has had a tap, so the menu's first move may be silent. Then open Options > Haptics and step Strength: each step plays a jump at that strength, and Off is silent. `?haptics` forces haptics on and `?haptics=off` forces them off for the visit, shown as "(link)" on the screen; neither is saved.
- **Over Wi-Fi** `bun run dev:phone` serves plain http. The spec does not appear to need a secure context for `vibrate`, but if nothing buzzes, plug the phone in and run `adb reverse tcp:5173 tcp:5173`, then open `http://localhost:5173/`, which is a secure context.
- **Check:** a menu move is a faint tick and Select a firmer one; Jump, a pogo bounce, Fizz, a stomp, a snack, getting hurt and the level win each feel different; nothing buzzes on the title's attract loop (only menu ticks do), a running buzz stops when the pause menu opens or the page hides; and the phone's Do Not Disturb silences it.
- **With a controller:** on a desktop, open `http://localhost:5173/?debug&level=0` in Chrome with a gamepad plugged in, press a button on the pad (so the game knows it is the device in use) and play: jumps, bonks, hurts and the level win should rumble the pad and not the screen. On a phone with a Bluetooth pad in Chrome for Android, say whether it rumbles at all.
- **Report back:** which phone and browser, whether the quietest cues (a Fizz shot, a menu move) can be felt at all, and which cues feel wrong. `__lf.debugHaptics().plays` lists what each cue compiled to.

## Tuning with the Haptics lab
The lab is for finding out what the phone and a controller can actually feel, and writing it down. Turn on Options > Haptics > Haptics lab (or open `/?debug&lab=haptics`), then tap the Lab button; with both labs reachable, the header's Sound | Haptics switch moves between them.
- **Cues:** tap a cue to feel it. The second line is its compiled length on the phone and the pad, at the lab's strength, and "silent" means it is under the floor.
- **Floor ladder** (Compare): pick Phone or Controller floor ladder and hold the phone as for play. Ten taps from 0.1 to 1.0 play 400 ms apart; the step where you first feel one is where `FLOOR` belongs. Set it on the Compile tab and run the ladder again.
- **Compare:** phone then controller three times, or a cue at 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0, shows whether Light, Medium and Strong feel different enough.
- **Tune:** pick a cue, change its events, cooldown, priority or policy; each change is live in the game too. Play and Play original are a quick A and B, and the bars show the pulses the phone gets and the segments the pad gets.
- **Compile:** `PERIOD`, `MIN_ON`, `MIN_OFF`, `FLOOR`, `T_BASE`, `T_SPAN`, the pad's minimum tap and segment length and the budget move the compilers and the budget at once.
- **Copy as JSON** copies only what moved (a changed cue whole) in the `__lf.debugHapticsTune` shape, for pasting into the console or into `fizz-haptics.ts` and `haptic-pattern.ts`. If the clipboard refuses, it is in the console. The lab's strength (0 to 1.5) is lab-only and never saved.

## CSS split
`ui.css` has grown to one file of about 1,500 lines organised only by comment banners, and slices 2 and 3 both edit it. The split goes right after slice 3 merges, so it does not collide with that work.
- **Pure move.** No selector, value or order changes. The entry file `@import`s the parts in their current order, so the cascade is identical, and the parts follow the surfaces that already exist. No barrel files: each file holds real rules.
- **Proof.** `bun run validate` and the whole Playwright suite (desktop and both touch projects, with the layout audit) pass unchanged, and a diff of the built CSS shows the same rules in the same order.
- **Process.** An Opus agent plans the split in plan mode when the slice starts (the file boundaries, the import order, and any rule that has to stay together). Sonnet agents then do the mechanical moves in small steps, with the audit run between steps.
- **Why now.** It makes touch and menu changes local to one file, lets a second episode reuse the shared menus, hint bar and touch parts without copying, and shrinks merge conflicts.

## Risks and how the plan handles them
- **Multi-touch ghosting and slide re-binding** are the most likely feel problems. The pure slide function and the multi-touch e2e come first (slices 1 and 2) so they are tuned before anything is built on them.
- **Visible area changes under the integer scale** (more tiles visible than 13). Slice 4 settled it as a view of at most 19.5 tiles (every level is at least 22 tall) with no bars, and its acceptance includes a playtest pass of the levels at phone sizes.
- **Secure-context features** (fullscreen, wake lock, the Gamepad API) are not available over plain LAN http. Slice 0 documents `adb reverse` and a Pages preview, and the code degrades silently.
- **iPhone Safari** has no Fullscreen API and no vibration. It is best effort: the game plays, without haptics, and the manifest and Add to Home screen give the cleanest result.
- **A touch button choosing a menu row by accident** is prevented by the fresh-press rule: a button already held when a menu opens chooses nothing until it is pressed again, and a held direction waits for a release.
- **Sub-frame taps** are prevented by the latch in slice 1.
- **Phones with a stale WebView** are covered by a device in the checklist and by keeping Soft available as the fallback.

## Decisions
- **Menus run on the controls (decided after the first phone test of slice 2).** The thumbs stay on the D-pad, Select (Jump) and Back (Pogo); taps are the backup. Menu content shifts right of the D-pad on touch, held directions auto-repeat in menus on touch and the keyboard, and the work lands as its own slice right after slice 2, ahead of pixel-perfect scale.
- **Touch mode follows the device in use.** It is on while the last-used device is touch: a coarse pointer at boot or any real touch turns it on, a key press or gamepad input turns it off (the controls hide and the desktop HUD returns), and the next touch brings it back. `?touch` pins it on for development and tests.
- **Touch HUD:** three glass pills with a sprite icon and a number (lives, snacks, fizz). "Next life at" is dropped on touch.
- **Pause fires on release** inside its hit area, so sliding off cancels it. Returning from portrait leaves the game on the pause menu.
- **Pogo state:** exported as a new read-only `State.POGO_ON` in slice 2.
- **Visible tile height:** under the integer scale the scale is `max(2, floor(H / (16 × target)))` and the tiles are whatever fills the height, with no bars (decided in slice 4, replacing the earlier 11 to 15 tile clamp with a margin).
- **Web manifest:** ships in the lifecycle slice, with the 2g icon exported at manifest sizes.
- **Browser Back:** intercepted only in fullscreen or installed mode.
- **Keep both Pause and Back on every menu (supersedes slice 5b's "drop Pause from sub-screens"; decided after the 5b phone test).** The on-screen Pause button stays up over every screen opened over a menu and leaves the whole menu; the Back button shows on the pause menu as well as over its sub-screens, and resumes there. Esc, P and Start still go back one level.
- **The one-column title stays the default on touch** (decided in slice 5): the game shows to the right of it and nothing takes over the screen. The split title is still opt-in (`?title=split`), and since slice 6.4 so are the split pause menu, Options, saves and cards, all through the one flag; slice 6 did not add a setting for it, and a Title layout row on the Touch controls screen stays an open question.

## Built in slice 5c
- **`BackGuard`** (`packages/engine/src/back-guard.ts`, exported as `./back-guard`): `new BackGuard(onBack, history, target)` with `set(want)`, `armed` and `dispose()`. While the game wants Back it holds exactly one history entry (`pushState({ lfBack: 1 }, '')`, same URL), never one per sub-screen. `set(false)` bumps a `skip` counter and calls `history.back()`; the `popstate` it causes is swallowed, and wants made while removals are landing are deferred (the latest wins). A user Back disarms the guard and calls `onBack`, then re-arms if the want still stands (a screen that absorbs Back keeps its entry). A page that opens on a leftover entry (a reload keeps the history state) clears it with `replaceState(null, '')`; `HistoryLike` lets the tests use a fake history. Reloading mid-game can still leave one extra entry, which is accepted.
- **`back.ts`** (episode, pure): `backAction(screen, sub)` is `null` for the title with nothing over it and for `loading` (the browser leaves the page), `close` for a screen over the title or pause menu, `pause` in play, `resume` on the pause menu, `skip` for `cine`, `credits` and `stinger`, and `none` for `dialogue`, `ending` and `card` (Back is absorbed). `backEnabled({ standalone, fullscreen, forced })` is true in an installed app (`display-mode` standalone, fullscreen or minimal-ui, or `navigator.standalone`), with an element fullscreen, or with `?back`.
- **Wiring.** `Game.back()` is public, so a native Back (the Tauri `predictive-back` plugin) can call it. `syncBack()` runs at the end of `syncUi` and holds the guard only while the mode allows it and `backAction` is not `null`; it re-runs on `fullscreenchange` and on `display-mode` changes. `?back` sets `GameOptions.back`, and `debugState.back` reports `{ enabled, armed }`. Back on the pause menu resumes, as Esc and Pause do.
- **Dormant on phones for now.** Fullscreen and the installed app arrive in slice 7, so on a phone browser the guard is off unless `?back` is used. On Android in fullscreen, Back fires no `popstate`; slice 7's auto-pause on `fullscreenchange` covers it.
- **Tests.** Unit tests for `BackGuard` (a fake history with delayed pops: arm, disarm, skip counting, deferral, the reload marker, no growth over many cycles), `backAction` and `backEnabled`; `e2e/back.spec.ts` (desktop, from `about:blank` with `/?debug&back`): Back pauses and resumes, closes Options, leaves from the title, five Options cycles add no history, quitting to the title disarms, and without `?back` the history is untouched. Playwright's `goBack` does not need a user gesture, so these runs do not show whether Chromium skips entries pushed without one; that needs a real device.

## Built in slice 6.1
- **`touch-settings.ts`** (engine, exported as `./touch-settings`): `TOUCH_KEY` (`lf-touch-v1`), `TouchSettings` (`size` S, M or L at 0.85, 1 and 1.2; `opacity` 40, 60, 85 or 100 percent, 85 by default so the controls look as they did; `leftHanded`; `hapticStrength`, 0 to 3, which replaced the `haptics` boolean in slice 8 H4 and reads an old `haptics: false` as 0; `pos`), `parseTouchSettings`, `serialiseTouchSettings`, `readTouchSettings`, `writeTouchSettings`, `touchSpec`, `withPosition` and `resetPositions`. Anything missing or invalid falls back per field; storage that throws, bad JSON, a non-object or any version other than 1 gives all defaults. Opacity snaps to the nearest step and a value halfway between two steps takes the lower one. `pos` keeps only `dpad`, `jump`, `pogo` and `fire` entries with finite `side` and `bottom` between 0 and 2000, rounded. Reading never writes back, and a failed write keeps the settings for the session.
- **Positions** are offsets in spec pixels from the control's own safe corner (`EdgeOffset`: the face edge's gap to the left edge for the D-pad and the right edge for the buttons, and to the bottom of the safe area), the same numbers `TouchSpec` holds for the default places. The Size scale multiplies them, Left-handed mirrors them, and a stored value is never rewritten when the window changes: it is clamped when it is placed.
- **Small stays at 48 dp.** `placeControls` floors the product of the height fit and the Size scale, so Small no longer draws faces under 48 dp below about 360 px tall.
- **Zones.** `controlZone` is the box a face centre may be in: on its own side, below `reserve.top` (96 px, which clears the HUD pills, the boss and toast lines and Pause), inside the safe area with `reserve.edge` (8 px) to spare, and leaving menus `reserve.content` (300 px). The D-pad's zone at 844×390 is x 91 to 181 and y 171 to 299. The default place is always allowed; when a side's default already needs more than half of the menu budget, the other side gives up what it needs, so the menu-set gutters never total more than `max(width - 300, the default total)`. `validPlacement` checks every hit area is inside the safe area, no hit areas overlap and the faces are at least `reserve.gap` (8 px) apart; if a custom layout fails it, the whole layout falls back to the defaults, and `PlacedControls.custom` says which is in use.
- **Dragging.** `offsetOf` is the inverse of placement; `dragControl` clamps a wanted centre into the zone and pushes it out of the other controls (Pause included) to the face gap, or stays where it was when boxed in.
- **`sideTops`** skips Pause by id (it skipped every face in the upper half, which would have ignored a raised D-pad) and no longer takes the window height.
- **`TouchControls.setSpec`** and `currentSpec`: a spec change places the controls again, and a changed placement releases held fingers.
- **Boot.** `Game` reads `lf-touch-v1` before building `TouchControls`, builds them with `touchSpec(settings)` and sets `--lf-touch-opacity` on `#touch`. `__lf.debugTouchSettings` returns the settings and `debugState.custom` says whether moved controls are in use. There is no screen to change them yet (6.2 and 6.3).

- **Which rows exist.** A row appears only where the page can do what it sets, decided once at boot from the capabilities found in 7.1 and 7.2: Fullscreen needs element fullscreen and a browser page (not the Tauri app), and Keep screen on needs a backend that can hold the screen (the browser's wake lock in a secure context, or the app's native one). So an iPhone has no Fullscreen row, plain LAN http has no Keep screen on row, and Options has no Display row when neither exists. A desktop browser has both: Auto resolves to off there, but On forces fullscreen on the next run start, and the wake lock helps gamepad players.
- **Storage.** `lf-ep1-options-v1` (still `v: 1`) gains `fullscreen` (0 Auto, 1 On, 2 Off; anything else reads as Auto) and `awake` (a boolean; anything else reads as On). A save from before them loads the defaults.
- **The address wins and is never saved.** `?fullscreen` (or `=on`) and `?fullscreen=off` fix Fullscreen, and `?wake` (or `=on`) and `?wake=off` fix Keep screen on. A fixed row shows `On (link)` or `Off (link)`, is dimmed, has no steppers and does not change when tapped; the screen opens on its first row that can change (Back when both are fixed). The saved choice stays in storage for when the link is gone. `UrlLocks` gained `fullscreen` and `wake` for this, and `debugState.lifecycle` gained `wakeWant` beside `fullscreenWant`; both report the setting in effect.
- **When a change takes effect.** Fullscreen is read each time a run starts or resumes, so a change applies from the next New game, Continue, Load or Resume; turning it Off never leaves fullscreen, it only stops asking. Keep screen on is read by the lifecycle policy, so turning it Off lets the screen go as soon as the policy next runs (the Display screen is itself a screen that does not keep the display on).
- **Code.** `display-options.ts` (pure, like `sound-options.ts`): `displayRows`, `displayShown`, `displayItems`, `stepDisplay`, `effectiveFullscreen`, `effectiveWake`, `displayLocked`, `displayLinkValue`. `Game` keeps `fullscreenUrl` and `wakeUrl` and reads the effective want through getters, so the policy always sees the current setting. `SubScreen` gained `display`, so the Back button, the touch faces and `pauseAction` treat it like the other screens opened over a menu. `__lf.debugShow('display')` opens it.
- **Not added.** No pause-on-blur row (blur always pauses a level in play, per the auto-pause table), no Reset row (there are two settings), no Install app row and no install UI (a 7.3 decision).
- **Layout cost.** On a touch device that can vibrate Options is now nine rows. They were drawn at 35 dp at 390 dp tall, 31 at 360 and 27 at 320 when this slice was built; they now keep their full row height (40 dp by default) and the list scrolls ("Built in menu scrolling"). The Display screen has three rows (four on a touch device, with Row spacing) and fits.
- **Tests.** Unit: `display-options.test.ts` (rows by capability, ids, the text on each row and the "(link)" rows, the effective settings, stepping and the locked rows), the new fields in `options.test.ts` (round trip, defaults, rejected values, stepping) and `display` in the `back` and `touchFaces` tables. Browser: `e2e/display-options.spec.ts` (the keyboard steps both rows and the settings are saved and used, Escape returns to the Display row, a saved Off is used at boot, `?fullscreen&wake=off` win over saved choices without writing) and `e2e/touch-display-options.spec.ts` (both phone projects: the row opens the screen, the layout audit, a stepper and a tap step the rows and save, and the Back button returns to Display). The Options label lists in the Sound, Haptics, Menus and Touch settings specs gained Display.
- **Not verified:** how any of this feels on a real phone.

## Built in menu scrolling
- **The rule.** On touch every menu row is `--lf-menu-row` tall: 40 dp by default (Cozy), 36 (Compact) or 48 (Comfy) from the Row spacing setting. The three heights are one table of named constants in `menu-density.ts`; `Ui.setMenuRow` sets the variable on the root, and `ui/touch-menus.css` keeps the 40 px default. Rows no longer shrink to fit. When a menu does not fit the height its screen leaves it, the menu scrolls inside its own area, and the head (the wordmark or heading), the hint bar, the Back button and the touch controls stay where they are. A menu that fits is untouched: nothing scrolls and nothing changes. It applies to every list drawn by the menu renderer (the title, Options and its sub-screens, Saves, Pause, the cards), in the column and in the split layout, and also to a desktop window too short for a list, where the mouse wheel scrolls it.
- **How it is built.** `Ui.fitRows` (`ui.ts`) clears each menu's height limit, measures how far its screen overflows, and sets the menu's `max-height` to its natural height less that overflow, then down to a whole number of rows (never under two; `snapViewport`), so a row is never shown half cut at the top or bottom edge; the strips for the cues are then reserved above and below it as the menu's margins, and half of what is still left goes above as spacing; `data-scroll` on the menu turns on `overflow-y: auto` (`ui/menus.css`), with the scrollbar hidden. The maths is pure and tested in `menu-scroll.ts`: `menuViewport` (the height to give), `stripLayout` (rows and strips in a given height), `chevronScale`, `uniformRowUnit`, `scrollCues` (which cues show), `snapScroll` (the nearest row boundary) and `revealRow` (the row-aligned offset that brings a row wholly into view), `rowVisible`, and `isTap` with `TAP_SLOP` (12 px; a finger that moves further is a drag, not a tap, so it scrolls and chooses nothing).
- **The cues.** A pixel-art chevron (an 11 × 7 art-pixel glyph, white with a one-pixel dark outline so it reads over the scrim and the orange plate; `chevronSvg`) shows at the bottom while rows are hidden below and, flipped, at the top while rows are hidden above; neither shows when nothing is hidden. Each stands centred on the menu in its own thin strip above or below the list, never in the row gutter, so it cannot collide with the selection cursor at either edge (the first design put it in the gutter beside the first or last visible row, where it covered the soda-can cursor of a selected edge row, on the title menu too). The strip is reserved whenever the menu scrolls (`Ui.layoutScroll` sets it as the menu's top and bottom margins, `data-strip` records it) so the layout does not jump as a cue comes and goes. `stripLayout` sizes it: the wanted height is half a row (20 dp at Cozy), paid for first from the pixels whole-row snapping frees, then shrunk towards a 10 dp minimum (`MIN_STRIP`), and a row is dropped only when even two minimum strips do not fit beside the rows left (never under two rows). The chevron is drawn at the largest whole scale the strip holds (`chevronScale`: the largest whole scale at which the 7-art-pixel glyph and the one-art-pixel step of its animation both fit, so 1 CSS pixel per art pixel up to a 17 dp strip, 2 from 18 and 3 from 27, never above the menu's own pixel size), steps one art pixel at a time (the cursor's rhythm) and does not move under the system reduced-motion setting or the Motion option. There is no scrollbar and no edge fade. **Cost:** where the pixels snapping frees are under two minimum strips, the list shows one row fewer than before the strips; across the title, Options, Pause and a long-slot Saves screen at 844×390, 740×360 and 640×320 in both layouts and the three densities, 19 of the 48 scrolling cases lost a row, mostly at Cozy and Comfy in the column (for example Options at 844×390 Cozy shows 5 of 9 rows, not 6). A one-column overlay now also keeps its bottom reserve at least as tall as a wrapped hint bar (`--lf-keys-h`), so a menu and its strip never run under a two-line hint.
- **Tall rows.** Snapping and the window maths assume equal row heights, so a taller row (a Save slot whose text wraps at a large Text size) used to leave a sliver of the next row at an edge. When a list scrolls, `Ui.fitRows` measures its rows once the menu is drawn (it runs again on every relayout, including once the Fizz font has loaded) and gives every row the height of the tallest (`--lf-menu-unit`, `uniformRowUnit`), never under the density's row height (`--lf-menu-row`), so the lists of one-line rows keep exactly their height and a menu that fits is not touched. In the column at 640×320 with the Large text size, Saves rows are 44 dp instead of 40.
- **Input.** The selected row is always scrolled into view when the selection changes (keys, the D-pad, a gamepad, wrapping from the last row to the first and back), moving by the fewest whole rows (the window always lands on a row boundary); a redraw keeps the scroll position otherwise. A mouse that hovers a row selects it without moving the list. On touch a drag scrolls natively (`touch-action: pan-y`), a tap chooses the row under the finger (hit-tested at the lift, so a scrolled list is right), and a drag that starts on a stepper changes nothing. The wheel scrolls a desktop list that overflows. **Snapping** is CSS: a scrolling menu has `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` and its rows `scroll-snap-align: start` (`ui/menus.css`), so a drag, a fling or the wheel comes to rest with a whole row at the top, and because the viewport is a whole number of rows the bottom edge falls between rows too. It is native, so it needs no script and has no timer; the browser's own settle motion is the only animation, and the game adds none (the chevrons already stop under reduced motion). Keys and the D-pad set the offset directly, to a row boundary. Checked in Chromium; Firefox for Android supports the same properties but has not been run here.
- **Layout audit.** `e2e/audit.ts` understands scroll containers: a row scrolled out of view is neither outside the window nor crowding the hints, a row cut by the menu's edge is judged by the part that shows, and new checks report a touch row shorter than `--lf-menu-row` (the chosen density's height), a selected row not wholly in view, and a scrolling menu or its cue strips overlapping the heading, the Back button, the hint bar or a touch control, and a shown chevron that is not centred inside its strip or touches a row or a row's cursor. `e2e/touch-menu-scroll.spec.ts` checks the nine-row Options screen at 844×390, 740×360 and 640×320 in both layouts, plus a drag and a tap on a scrolled row, no row cut at either edge after keys, a drag and at the bottom, the three densities' row heights, Row spacing resizing the open Display screen and holding after a reload, Saves with long wrapping slots (one row unit, no sliver after keys, a drag or at the bottom), and the title menu with the first, last and middle rows selected.
- **The Controls table** is a table, not a menu list, and is unchanged: it trims its cell padding at 640×320 and hides its note and the wordmark on a short touch screen.
- **Row spacing.** Options > Display > Row spacing (touch devices only; Compact 36, Cozy 40 default, Comfy 48) is the `density` option, 0, 1 or 2, parsed in `parseOptions` (anything else falls back to Cozy; a save without it is Cozy; the storage key is unchanged). `display-options.ts` has the row and its stepping; `Game.applySettings` calls `Ui.setMenuRow`, so the open screen re-lays out as the value steps. On a touch device the Display row therefore always exists, even where Fullscreen and Keep screen on do not. There is no `?density` link override: it would be one more `UrlLocks` field and a "(link)" row for a setting that is only a preference. The stepper's width is `max(40px, --lf-menu-row)` so Compact's longest value ("Compact") still fits the narrowest column at 640 px wide. 36 dp is above the audit's minimum and the 22 px glyph cell fits it with 7 px above and below, so the text is legible; the target is smaller than Android's 48 dp, which is why Comfy exists.
- **Not built:** a `?density` link override, and a keyboard or D-pad "page" step.

## Built in slice 7.6
- **What it does.** A round glass button with a pixel-art glyph sits in the top-right corner: four corner brackets pointing outward (Expand) while the page is not fullscreen, and pointing inward (Collapse) while it is. Its accessible name is `Fullscreen` or `Exit fullscreen` (`aria-label` and `title`). Pressing it, or pressing `F`, enters element fullscreen, or leaves it when the page is already in it. It is an explicit action: it works whatever Options > Display > Fullscreen says and under `?fullscreen=off`, which govern only the automatic requests when a run starts or resumes. Auto is unchanged: it is on for touch devices only, so a desktop never goes fullscreen on its own, and nothing about the button nags.
- **Where it shows** (`fullscreenButton` in `lifecycle-policy.ts`, pure). Touch: the title and the pause menu with every screen opened over them (Options and its sub-screens, Saves, Controls), and not in play (play already goes fullscreen on its gesture, and the corner is Pause's), not on the level cards and not on the scenes or the touch editor. Desktop: the title, the pause menu, the level cards and play, small, with nothing automatic about it. It is hidden where element fullscreen cannot work in a browser page, which is the rule the Display screen uses (`caps.fullscreen` and not the app): an iPhone and the Tauri app. A page that booted in an installed display mode still shows it; the rule does not look at `caps.installed`.
- **Placement.** On touch it is the Pause pill's size and style: a 48 dp hit area and a 36 dp face, 8 dp in from the corner of the safe area, with the lighter glass and firm outline the controls take on a menu. It sits in the corner alone on the title's top level (Pause is not shown there) and 4 dp to the left of Pause's hit area (x 736 to 784 at 844 wide, y 8 to 56) wherever Pause shows; Left-handed keeps both at the top right, with Back at the top left. On a desktop it is a 32 px circle 16 px in from the corner, and the engine panel button and the Lab button of `?debug` move left by 40 px while it shows. The CSS is `ui/fullscreen-button.css`; the glyphs (`GLYPHS`, 9 by 9, white) and the small `FullscreenControl` are in `fullscreen-button.ts`, and the game owns the one instance and mounts it through `Ui.mount`.
- **The request.** `Game.toggleFullscreen()` runs inside the click or the keydown, so the request keeps its user gesture. Entering reuses `enterFullscreen` (and records `lastFs`, `scaleBefore` and `scaleAfter` as the automatic request does); on a touch device it also locks the landscape orientation. Leaving is the new `exitFullscreen(doc)` in `lifecycle.ts`, which calls `document.exitFullscreen()` before any `await` and never throws. `gestureFor` and the "first tap goes fullscreen" idea are untouched: the button replaces that rule.
- **The `F` decision.** `F` is free in both layouts: movement is the arrows and `WASD`, Fizz is Space or `C`, Jump is Ctrl or `Z`, Pogo is Alt or `X`, and the other bindings are Esc, `P`, Enter, F5, F9, the backtick, `-`, `=` and `0`, so no key was moved. Ctrl+F, Alt+F (Alt is Pogo) and Meta+F are ignored, a held key does not repeat (the key-down edge only, `isFullscreenKey` in `input.ts`), and a key typed into an `input`, `textarea` or `select` never reaches it. The key emits a `fullscreen` command from the keydown handler, and `Game` acts on it synchronously, so the request is still inside the gesture. It works wherever the button shows, which on a touch screen leaves play out (a keyboard press switches the game to the desktop rules anyway).
- **Hints and Controls.** Where the page can go fullscreen (`caps.fullscreen` and not the app), a keyboard's hint bar ends with `F Fullscreen` (not on the Controls screen, whose table lists it and which has no room for a third hint on a narrow window) and the desktop Controls table gains a Fullscreen row (`F`, `F`, and a dash under Gamepad); on a short window (700 px tall or less) that table closes its rows up by their padding so the extra row still fits. The touch Controls table gains `Fullscreen` and `Fullscreen button`, with no keyboard glyph; the touch and gamepad hint bars are unchanged. With none of that available (an iPhone, the app) the hints and tables are exactly as before. On a touch screen the first key press switches the hints to the keyboard's, and the extra `F Fullscreen` entry can wrap a narrow hint bar to a second line, so the shell measures the bar (`--lf-keys-h`, on the title as it already was on the overlay screens) and the menu is refitted above it; a scrolling list then has a row or two fewer after that key press.
- **Interactions.** Leaving fullscreen in play still pauses the level (7.1), so the button or `F` pausing a level it just left is the same rule, and Esc leaving fullscreen is the browser's: no extra pause or Back comes from it. The `fullscreenchange` handler that re-syncs the Back guard also re-syncs the button, so the glyph, the name and the guard follow however fullscreen ends. The click blurs the button so Space and Enter stay the game's, and the button is outside the Tab order, which the game keeps. The layout re-derives on the resulting resize as it does for the automatic request. No `debugState` field was added: the button is read from the DOM (`#fsBtn`, `data-glyph`, `aria-label`) and `lifecycle.lastFs` covers the request.
- **Tests.** Unit: `fullscreenButton` over every screen kind on touch and desktop, with and without element fullscreen and in the app, and its glyph and label; `exitFullscreen` with fakes (called at once, nothing to leave, missing, throwing and rejecting); `isFullscreenKey` (F alone, repeat, Ctrl, Alt, Meta, other keys, and no game bit for F); the hints and Controls tables with and without the shortcut (touch with no key glyph); the glyph grids. Browser, desktop (`e2e/fullscreen-button.spec.ts`): the button at the top right with its name; a click and `F` enter and leave real fullscreen (headless Chromium allows it) and flip the glyph; a spy shows one `requestFullscreen` per press and a held key toggles once; Ctrl+F, Alt+F and a focused `input` do nothing; Fullscreen Off and `?fullscreen=off` still allow it; nothing is fullscreen on its own; play shows it and leaving pauses; the scenes hide it; `?host=app` and a missing `fullscreenEnabled` hide it and drop the hint and the Controls row; the hint bar and Controls row; no overlap with the engine panel or Lab buttons; the Back guard follows. Browser, touch (`e2e/touch-fullscreen-button.spec.ts`, both phone projects plus 640×320, column and `?title=split`, over the title, Options and the pause menu): at least 48 dp, inside a notch and bar inset, beside Pause (alone on the title), clear of Back, the head, the rows, the hints and the controls, and the layout audit finds nothing the screen did not already have; absent in play, on cards, on scenes and in the editor; a tap enters and leaves fullscreen and starts nothing; Fullscreen Off and `?fullscreen=off` do not stop it; Left-handed; hidden in the app and without element fullscreen; the touch Controls table.
- **Menu scrolling.** The button sits in the top-right corner, outside the scrolling menu, its strips and the hint bar, so it does not change how a menu is fitted ("Built in menu scrolling"); the touch spec checks the layout audit, which now covers the scroll containers, with the button shown.
- **Not verified:** real fullscreen on a phone (the request inside a tap, the landscape lock and the resize on Android Chrome and Firefox), how an installed app behaves with the button, and how it feels under a thumb. Headless Chromium honours the Fullscreen API, which is what the browser specs use, but that is not a device.

## Open questions
- **Make the split the default?** It is opt-in (`?title=split`) for the title and, since 6.4, for every menu. It gives 48 px rows on the title, the pause menu and the saves at 844×390 and 740×360 (the column gives 35 to 41), and Options 39 against 30. The cost is that the game behind the menu is covered on the thumb side and the heading floats at the far edge. A Title layout row (next) could make it a choice, or it could become the default with the column as the fallback it already is.
- **Per-hand layouts.** Left-handed mirrors one layout. A player who moves the controls for one hand and then switches gets the mirror image, not a separate layout; whether the two should be stored apart is open.
- **Nudge buttons for TalkBack.** Dragging is not reachable with a screen reader. Reset and the defaults are the fallback; arrow or nudge buttons in the editor are planned for slice 9.
- **A Title layout row.** `?title=split` is still the only way to the split title and the split menus; a row on the Touch controls screen would be one more field in `lf-touch-v1` (`title`), with the URL flag overriding it, and would switch every menu together.
- **Rows below 48 dp on short phones.** Settled by "Built in menu scrolling": rows keep their height and the list scrolls, the default is 40 dp and Options > Display > Row spacing offers 36 and 48. What stays open is which default feels right on a real phone; the three heights are one table in `menu-density.ts`.
- **A second tap for destructive rows** (Quit to title, Leave level), as MOBILE.md describes, is not built: those rows act on one tap, as they do with a key.
