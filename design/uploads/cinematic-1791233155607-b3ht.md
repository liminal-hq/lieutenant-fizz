Ben Blaze is on a quest after his Keen cousin isn't one one night and he finds a prototype spagetty with meatballs flying saucer in a secret lab under the treehouse they always play in. He notices a note Billy left behind with a map and an X with a planet name beside it. "That must be where he's gone" as Ben puts on his bicycle helmet and hits the Engage button in the flying saucer and is off 

The G-force pressed Ben deep into the oversized, noodle-upholstered captain’s chair as the prototype spaghetti with meatballs flying saucer tore through the stratosphere. Marinara-scented exhaust plumed behind him, painting a crimson streak across the night sky. He gripped the steering yoke—which felt suspiciously like a giant, hardened breadstick—and steadied his breathing. His bicycle helmet rattled against the headrest, a far cry from the sturdy football helmet his cousin Billy usually wore, but it would have to do for interstellar travel.

On the dashboard, pinned beneath a blinking, meatball-shaped radar dial, was the note Billy had left behind. Ben tapped the X on the crinkled parchment. The destination was boldly scribbled as *Planet Zargoth*, a remote outpost in the outer galactic rim. It was exactly the kind of dangerous, alien world Billy would wander off to, perfectly matching the bizarre blueprints and planetary coordinates Ben had once glimpsed in the "Commander Keen" file Billy kept hidden on his computer.

As the ship broke free of Earth's gravity, the artificial gravity compensators kicked in with a soft, bubbling hum. Ben engaged the autopilot and took a moment to gather his nerves. Space was vastly darker and much colder than the treehouse ever was. To keep his energy up for whatever alien weirdness awaited him, he popped open the saucer's glovebox and pulled out his carefully stashed emergency rations: a crinkled bag of Hawkins Cheezies and a cold can of Canadian cream soda. The loud, satisfying crunch of the bright orange snacks echoed in the quiet cabin, offering a tiny, comforting reminder of home while the stars stretched into streaks of blinding white light outside the viewport.

Hours later, the navigation console chimed with a whimsical, retro 8-bit melody. *Planet Zargoth* loomed through the front windshield, a swirling marble of neon green and deep purple clouds. Ben wiped his cheesy fingers on his jeans, grabbed the control yoke, and manoeuvred the pasta-themed vessel through the upper atmosphere. The ship rattled violently, meatball-thrusters firing in rapid succession to slow their descent toward the rocky surface.

He touched down with a heavy, saucy thud in the centre of a glowing, crystalline forest. Popping the main hatch, the ramp extended with a hiss of pressurized steam. Ben adjusted the chin strap of his bicycle helmet, tucked a trusty, modified pogo stick under one arm, and stepped out into the alien unknown, determined to track down his missing cousin.
